\begin{abstract}
Given an odd prime $p$, a quadratic nonresidue is a nonzero residue which
is not a square modulo $p$.  We give a deterministic construction using
\[
 \exp\!\bigl(O(\sqrt{\log p\log\log(p+16)})\bigr)
\]
bit operations, with effective absolute constants.  The algorithm does
not require a factorization of $p-1$.  It tests a rigorously specified
finite list of rational integers and modular images of units and small
principal-ideal generators in quadratic and biquadratic fields.
A sparse positive-semidefinite Gram average, including ramified primes,
forces one negative character value; a uniform varying-field Burgess
estimate supplies the contradiction if none exists.  This manuscript
contains the complete compatible-root construction, parameter choice,
enumeration algorithm, character assembly, and analytic proof.  Its
certificate is simply Euler's criterion, so the output is independently
verified by one modular exponentiation.
\end{abstract}

\section{Statement and an elementary certificate}

For an odd prime $p$, the quadratic character is
\[
 \chi_p(a)=
 \begin{cases}
 0,&p\mid a,\\
 1,&a\not\equiv0\pmod p\text{ is a square},\\
 -1,&a\text{ is a nonsquare}.
 \end{cases}
\]
Euler's criterion says
$a^{(p-1)/2}\equiv\chi_p(a)\pmod p$ for $a\ne0$.
Thus a proposed nonresidue has a short, deterministic certificate:
modular exponentiation must return $p-1$.
For example, $3^3\equiv-1\pmod7$, so 3 is a nonresidue modulo 7.
The issue is not verification; it is finding a successful candidate
without an unproved hypothesis about the first small nonsquare.

All unqualified logarithms are natural, and
$\operatorname{bitlen}(p)=\lfloor\log_2p\rfloor+1$.

\begin{theorem}[Deterministic nonresidue construction]
\label{thm:qnr-main}
Given an odd prime $p$ in binary, one can deterministically produce
$a\in\{1,\ldots,p-1\}$ with $\chi_p(a)=-1$ in
\begin{equation}
 \exp\!\bigl(O(\sqrt{\log p\log\log(p+16)})\bigr)
 \label{eq:qnr-cost}
\end{equation}
bit operations.  The implied constant is effective and absolute.
No factorization of $p-1$, generalized Riemann hypothesis, or
unproved distribution assumption is used.
\end{theorem}

This is the nonresidue specialization of a sparse ideal-character
construction.  In the pinned deterministic Cogentic source
\cite{CogDet2026}, the corresponding bound is
$\exp(O(\sqrt{\log p}\log\log p))$.
The difference here is the use of a sparse triangle-compatible Gram
family and the retention of all ramified small primes.
With
$L_p[\alpha,c]=\exp((c+o(1))(\log p)^\alpha
(\log\log p)^{1-\alpha})$, the new scale is
$L_p[1/2,O(1)]$.  No optimized leading $L$-constant is asserted.

\section{Compatible roots without knowing a nonresidue}
\label{sec:qnr-roots}

It might appear circular to enumerate number fields modulo $p$ when a
square-root algorithm often starts by finding a nonresidue.  The
construction below avoids that circularity: it creates square roots of
\emph{selected products} of small signed primes, using only two-group
comparisons.  It never calls a general square-root routine.

Write $p-1=2^tu$ with $u$ odd; this only strips trailing binary zeroes.
Let $q_0=-1$ and let $q_1,\ldots,q_K<p$ be distinct positive primes.
First test all $q_j$ by Euler's criterion.  A negative value ends the
algorithm.  Otherwise put $z_j=q_j^u\pmod p$ and find its order
$2^{r_j}$ by repeated squaring.  Choose a pivot $j_*$ of maximal
$r_j$, and set $\zeta=z_{j_*}$, $r_*=r_{j_*}$.
Because $z_0=-1$, $r_*\geq1$.

To recover $m_j$ with $z_j=\zeta^{m_j}$, start with $m=0$ and maintain
\[
 (z_j\zeta^{-m})^{2^{r_*-\nu}}=1\pmod p.
\]
At step $\nu$, its half-power is one of the two field roots $\pm1$ of
$X^2-1$.  In the $+1$ case leave $m$ unchanged; in the $-1$ case add
$2^\nu$.  This flips the half-power because
$\zeta^{2^{r_*-1}}=-1$.  At the end $z_j=\zeta^{m_j}$, with
$0\leq m_j<2^{r_*}$.
This proof only uses the two roots of $X^2-1$ in an odd prime field;
there is no hidden discrete-logarithm search of exponential length.

Let $i_1,\ldots,i_K$ be the nonpivot indices.  Define
\[
 \delta_r=m_{i_r}\bmod2,\qquad
 d^{(r)}=q_{j_*}^{\delta_r}q_{i_r},\qquad
 s^{(r)}=(d^{(r)})^{(u+1)/2}
          \zeta^{-(m_{i_r}+\delta_r)/2}\pmod p.
\]
Then $(s^{(r)})^2=d^{(r)}\pmod p$.  For a binary vector $\ell$, write
$\sum_r\ell_r\delta_r=2k(\ell)+\epsilon(\ell)$ and put
\begin{equation}
 d_\ell=q_{j_*}^{\epsilon(\ell)}\prod_{\ell_r=1}q_{i_r},
 \qquad
 s_\ell=q_{j_*}^{-k(\ell)}\prod_{\ell_r=1}s^{(r)}\pmod p.
 \label{eq:qnr-compatible-roots}
\end{equation}
The nonzero vectors give distinct nontrivial signed squarefree
radicands, and $s_\ell^2=d_\ell$.
For $\ell_3=\ell_1+\ell_2$, cancellation gives
\[
 s_{\ell_1}s_{\ell_2}=\eta_{12}s_{\ell_3}\pmod p,
 \qquad
 d_{\ell_3}=d_{\ell_1}d_{\ell_2}/\gcd(|d_{\ell_1}|,|d_{\ell_2}|)^2,
\]
where $|\eta_{12}|$ is the gcd and its sign is negative exactly when
both radicands are negative.  This is the compatibility condition for
the corresponding biquadratic modular embedding.

If $q_K\leq R$, vectors of weight at most two give
$|d_\ell|\leq R^3$.  Only those vectors will be used.
The base construction costs
$O(K(\log p)^4)$ elementary bit operations, including powering and
two-group comparisons; each requested sparse root needs only
polynomially many further bit operations.  In particular the
$2^K$ possible vectors are never enumerated.

\section{The finite candidate family}
\label{sec:qnr-family}

For an integer $m\geq1$ set
\begin{equation}
 L=2^m,\quad R=2^{\lceil\sqrt{256Lm}\rceil},\quad
 K=\pi(R),\quad S=\{0,e_1,\ldots,e_K\},\quad H=K+1.
 \label{eq:qnr-scale}
\end{equation}
These parameters use exact integer square roots and powers of two.
Every edge difference of $S$ has weight at most two.
By Section~\ref{sec:qnr-roots}, its field has discriminant at most
$4R^3$.  Each triangle compositum has discriminant at most
\[
 D_*=256R^{12},
\]
using the order basis
$1,\sqrt{d_1},\sqrt{d_2},\sqrt{d_1d_2}$.
Only primes at most $R$ ramify in these fields.

For every field use its actual discriminant to define
$M_F=\lceil\sqrt{|\Delta_F|}\rceil$ or
$M_E=\lceil\sqrt{|\Delta_E|}\rceil$, and set
\[
 Z_F=\max(M_F^3,M_FR),\qquad
 Z_E=\max\bigl(M_E^3,M_E\max_{F\subset E}M_F^2\bigr).
\]
The effective integer guard
\begin{equation}
 B_m=64D_*^4(R+1)
 \label{eq:qnr-guard}
\end{equation}
dominates $R$, all norm cutoffs, and 64 times every field discriminant.
At a scale with $p>B_m$, all hypotheses of
Theorem~\ref{thm:qp-enumeration} hold with $N=p$.
That algorithm cannot return a proper factor of the prime input, so it
returns the required lists of unit and principal-generator images.

The candidates are $-1$, the integers $1,\ldots,R$, and all images
from every edge and triangle field.  Test them by Euler's criterion.
Their number and construction cost are
\begin{equation}
 \exp(O(\log R))\operatorname{poly}(\log p),
 \label{eq:qnr-family-cost}
\end{equation}
since there are at most $H^2$ edge occurrences and $H^3$ triangles,
and each fixed-degree enumeration has polynomial cost in its numerical
discriminant and cutoff.  Testing an image is a single modular
exponentiation of bit cost $\operatorname{poly}(\log p)$.

\section{The contradiction if every candidate is a square}
\label{sec:qnr-proof}

Suppose $L\leq\log p<2L$, $p>B_m$, and every candidate has
character one.  Triviality on the listed unit generators implies
triviality on every unit.  A generator of a principal ideal differs
from a listed generator by a unit, so all principal-generator
hypotheses of Lemma~\ref{lem:qp-assembly} hold as well.
We obtain compatible multiplicative ideal characters in the edge and
triangle fields.  At all primes at most $R$, including ramified primes,
the small-prime values are one.

Write $X=p^{1/3}$, $V=\log R$, and
\[
 u_0=\lceil(\log X)/V\rceil,
 \qquad Y_1=\lfloor X^{1/u_0}\rfloor\leq R.
\]
Lemma~\ref{lem:qp-explicit-scale} verifies the following inequalities
throughout the explicit conservative range $m\geq4096$.
The elementary prime-product bound gives
\begin{equation}
 \Psi_p(X,R)\geq X(25\log X)^{-u_0}\geq Xe^{-V/8}.
 \label{eq:qnr-smooth}
\end{equation}
Indeed $u_0\leq2L/(3V)+1$ and
$V^2\geq256(\log2)L\log L$, so
$u_0\log(25\log X)/V<1/8$ eventually.
There are enough primes in $(Y_1/2,Y_1]$, and all are coprime to $p$.
Also $H-1=\pi(R)\geq cR/\log R$ gives
\[
 \frac{X(1+\log X)}{H-1}\leq Xe^{-3V/4}.
\]
The ramification-tolerant Gram inequality
\eqref{eq:qp-gram-average} therefore supplies an edge field with ideal
sum at least $\frac12Xe^{-V/8}$.

Every edge discriminant obeys $D\leq4R^3$, so
$D\leq p^{1/200}$ eventually, since $V=o(\log p)$.
The least-prime condition is simply $p>D$ and is ensured by the guard.
The uniform orbit estimate and class summation in
Corollary~\ref{cor:qp-ideal-sum} give
\[
 \left|\sum_{N\mathfrak a\leq X}\chi_F^*(\mathfrak a)\right|
 \leq C_BD^3Xp^{-1/4000}
 \leq Xe^{-(\log p)/8000}
\]
on that range.
Because $p>X$, there are no ideals of norm at most $X$ divisible by
the rational prime $p$, so this is the same sum as in the Gram bound.
The two estimates contradict each other.  Thus some enumerated image
is a nonresidue.  Its actual value is found by the Euler tests; the proof
does not require computing the ideal character or the witnessing
number field's class group.

\section{A total algorithm and its bit complexity}

\begin{algorithm}[Deterministic quadratic nonresidue]
\label{alg:qnr-full}
Input an odd prime $p$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Test $p-1$ by Euler's criterion and return it if negative.
      Put $n=\operatorname{bitlen}(p)$ and form the guards
      \eqref{eq:qnr-guard} for
      $1\leq m\leq\lfloor\log_2n\rfloor$.
      Let $B_{\max}$ be their maximum, or 2 for an empty list.
\item If $B_{\max}\geq p$, scan $a=1,\ldots,p-1$ with Euler's
      criterion and return the first nonresidue.
\item Otherwise, at each scale construct the base roots as in
      Section~\ref{sec:qnr-roots}, returning any nonresidue detected
      there.  Enumerate all edge and triangle candidate images of
      Section~\ref{sec:qnr-family}; test and return the first negative
      Euler value.
\item If all scales fail, finish by scanning $a=1,\ldots,p-1$.
\end{enumerate}
Reduce every returned residue to $\{1,\ldots,p-1\}$ and verify its
Euler value equals $-1$.
\end{algorithm}

Half of the nonzero residues are nonsquares: the squaring map on
$\mathbb F_p^*$ has kernel $\{1,-1\}$.  Hence the scans terminate.
For all sufficiently large $p$, $B_{\max}<p$ because
$\log B_{\max}=O(\sqrt{n\log(n+2)})=o(n)$.
The scale $m_* =\lfloor\log_2\log p\rfloor$ is included and tends
to infinity.  Section~\ref{sec:qnr-proof} makes its candidate list
succeed whenever $m_*\geq4096$.  Thus the last scan is used on a
finite explicitly bounded range only; no large threshold needs to be
computed or stored by the algorithm.
The first scan has cost at most
$B_{\max}\operatorname{poly}(\log p)$ whenever it is invoked.

There are $O(\log n)$ scales.  At every one,
$\log R=O(\sqrt{n\log(n+2)})$; formula
\eqref{eq:qnr-family-cost} bounds the full candidate construction and
testing cost.  Polynomial logarithmic factors are absorbed into its
exponential.  This proves Theorem~\ref{thm:qnr-main}.

\section{What the result does and does not provide}

The output certificate is elementary and independent of the
construction: an arbitrary implementation can check the answer by
Euler's criterion.  The substantial work in the proof is a guaranteed
small enough candidate family, not that last verification.
The argument supplies a first $L$-parameter of $1/2$; it does not prove a
polylogarithmic bound for the least positive nonresidue, and the output
need not be a small rational integer.  It may be the modular image of a
unit or of a principal-ideal generator in an auxiliary field.

The provided exact verification programs check finite algebraic
identities and exponent accounting.  They are not an implementation of
all numerical-discriminant enumeration steps or evidence of practical
speed.  All effective constructions and their bit costs are specified
in the following appendix.  No formal verification of this entire
manuscript is claimed.
