## 1. Analytic range checks

The important imports were checked against their actual statements, including the following page images and exact theorem versions.

- Drappeau, *Sommes friables d'exponentielles et applications*, Canad. J. Math. 67 (2015), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.4153/CJM-2014-036-5), Proposition 2.9 p.618 and Proposition 2.13 p.626. The latter is the exceptional-character estimate at the actual primitive exceptional conductor, not a generic zero-free assertion. With \(L=\log x,\ Y'=\log y,\ u=L/Y'\asymp d\), we have \(Y'\gg\sqrt L\) (indeed \(Y'^2/L\gg\log\log N\)) and \(u\gg(\log L)^5\). For the nonexceptional case use height \(T=\exp(h)\) with \(h=\min(aY'/\log_3 x,\sqrt L)\). The cutoff \(\log Q_c=O(u^{3/4})=o(h)\) allows the strip at \(1-\epsilon,\ \epsilon\asymp1/h\), and the contour bound is \(O((\log x)^2(he^{-cL/h}+e^{-ch}))\). Both exponents dominate \(u^{3/4}\). In the exceptional case the factor \(\sqrt{q_1}/\phi(q_1)\) cancels the Gauss factor in W; the \(H(u)^{-\delta}\) term and the three error terms all decay faster than \(\exp(-cu^{3/4})\). The source also requires \(y\le\exp(L/(\log L)^4)\), which follows from \(u\gg(\log L)^5\). The proof does not need a common exceptional conductor at different endpoints.
- Harper, *Minor arcs, mean values, and restriction theory for exponential sums over smooth numbers*, Compos. Math. 152 (2016), [accepted manuscript, p.23, Prop. 2](https://wrap.warwick.ac.uk/id/eprint/107457/7/WRAP-minor-arcs-mean-values-restriction-theory-exponential-sums-over-smooth-numbers-Harper-2018.pdf). The exponent is \(2+20(1-\beta)+\epsilon\) and its explicit cutoff is \(x^{-1/1000}<\delta\le1\). For the large-value portion, layer cake with \(p=12/5\) works because \(\beta\to1\). For the small-value portion, \(\sum |S_x|^2\ll x\Psi(x,y)\) at \(1/x\)-separated frequencies, so their normalized p-energy is \(O(x^{-1/2500}/\rho)=o(1)\). This independently removes the unnecessarily restrictive guard \(K_{\max}^2\ll x^{1/1000}\). Gauss orthogonality costs \(1/r\) times additive energy, and Hölder gives the dyadic conductor exponent \(1-4/p=-2/3\), not \(-1\). Taking moving endpoint suprema uses one bounded random-sign sequence at each binary level, not different coefficients for different characters in Harper's theorem.
- Hildebrand–Tenenbaum, [survey, Thm. 5.1](https://www.numdam.org/item/JTNB_1993__5_2_411_0/), and the 1986 ratio/saddle theorem. The direct interval lower length \(xy^{-5/12}\) is satisfied by the retained relative mesh \(\exp(-D^{1/6}(\log\log N)^2-O(A\log D))\). Dividing a cell by a content, parent or avoidance divisor does not reduce its relative mesh. All retained endpoints have log-size \(\asymp d\log Y\) and smoothness argument \(\asymp d\). The rational side's much larger endpoint range does not justify replacing the rational saddle by the algebraic one; the conductor proof keeps both saddles separate.

These checks are of the imported statements and their use. They do not reprove the classical contour and restriction theorems.

## 2. Finite distribution and covariance audit

### Two-point window

Let two primitive nonproportional integer vectors \(P_1,P_2\) have determinant of absolute value \(\Delta\). Let \(\Gamma_m\) be the image of degree-m homogeneous integer forms under the two evaluations. A unimodular variable change maps \(P_2\) to \((1,0)\), \(P_1\) to \((u,\pm\Delta)\) with \((u,\Delta)=1\). The gcd of the monomial evaluations divisible by \(\Delta\) is exactly \(\Delta\), so \(\Gamma_m=\{(x,y):x\equiv u^m y\bmod\Delta\}\) for \(m\ge1\). It has index \(\Delta\). For a primitive linear form G vanishing on \((r,Q)\), write \(G_i=G(P_i)\ne0\). The map
\[
 \Gamma_d\longrightarrow\mathbb Z/(|G_1G_2|),\qquad
 (f(P_1),f(P_2))\longmapsto f(r,Q)
\]
is well-defined because the integer kernel consists of multiples of the product of the two primitive vanishing forms (Gauss). It is surjective because \((r,Q)=1\). Its kernel is precisely \(\operatorname{diag}(G_1,G_2)\Gamma_{d-1}\): subtract a kernel polynomial whose evaluation cancels \(f(r,Q)\), then divide by the primitive G. This verifies the cyclic quotient needed to uniformize w in B.4, including common prime divisors of \(G_1,G_2\). It is not a product of two independent uniform residues. Since \(\widehat F(r,Q)=N\) is coprime to this quotient, w times \(\widehat F\) generates it.

When \(\Delta\) is even the two odd values of \(F_0\) need not be units modulo \(\Delta\). The source's choice of the odd multiplier lift modulo \(2\Delta\), using \((F_0(P_1)-u^d F_0(P_2))/\Delta\), does not invert either value. This is necessary. For a common odd root divisor q, dividing the actual odd affine coset by q leaves modulus \(2\Delta/q\), not the squarefree part or \(\Delta/q\). The finite quotient and parity steps admit independent exact checks.

### Principal projection and content

The shared capped variable really is \(V_*=\gcd(u,\gcd(Q_o,K_o))\). Under the ideal CRT law, at every prime one of the two valuations is a function of the other; conditional on the smaller cap, the remaining tail is independent of every other prime. Thus \(S,T\) are conditionally independent given \(V_*\). For the extended actual-law test \(0\le f\le1\), this gives \(E_T E_S f=E_{V_*}f\); it does not assert independence of f itself. The relative finite-pattern estimates must be applied to nonnegative retained numerators before replacing the actual law by the ideal one: otherwise division by the smooth density after a fourth-moment estimate is invalid. The U100 §4 and U40 §3.3 proofs do use the relative comparison first.

For a complete semismooth parent band J, the exceptional prime \(\ell\mid K_o\), \(\ell>y\), supports its individual term only on the event \(v_\ell(T)=1\). At cap one this event includes higher valuations in the denominator; at higher cap it enforces exact valuation one. The conditional density's fourth norm can be as large as \(O(c_\ell\ell^{-1/4})\), not \(O(c_\ell/\ell)\). U40 (S7) correctly uses the larger bound. The number of these primes is at most \(\log K/\log P\), and the normalized sum relative to the complete-band reference is \(O((\log K)P^{-1/4})=o(1)\). This is a genuine use of the complete-band population; arbitrary sparse parent weights would not be covered.

### Nonprincipal and boundary terms

The semismooth character expansion must split according to whether the **combined** content/induction divisor h is smooth, contains exactly one allowed parent to exponent one, or contains an incompatible large prime. Expanding only h before induction and forgetting a parent occurring in the induction divisor is incorrect. U40 (S8)–(S10) handles the combined divisor, preserving the \(r/k\) factor. Since \((1-\beta_F)\log Y=O(\log D)\), the extra semismooth sum contributes only a polynomial factor in D after normalization by \(c_J\). The rational side's induction support restriction \(q\nmid he\) still holds for a queried child q below the next cutoff, because q>B and the rational numerator is B-smooth.

The common determinant must be kept in the nonnegative majorant. For each q-band the lcm summation
\[
 \sum_{L\mid t,\ t\le T}1/t\le(1+\log T)/L
\]
is applied with \(L=\mathrm{lcm}(H_1r/(r,q),H_2s/(s,2))\). This removes at most one q and one 2, not their full powers. The residual factor \(\sqrt{(r,q)}\) is bounded on average over a complete q-band by
\(1+\sqrt{2U}\omega_{(U,2U]}(r)/|\mathcal J|\), which is bounded by a fixed polynomial in D and in fact \(1+o(1)\) in our range. The sum over contents converges for each saddle >3/4. The rational-principal companion is controlled by \(\sum_{h\mid t}h^{1-2\beta_r}\), which is polylogarithmic by splitting its Euler product at \((\log(3T))^2\). The rational point supply normalization is canceled **pointwise on the common environment event** before taking this average. No independent distribution of the rational and algebraic modulus is invoked.

The density-normalized boundary error is not obtained by dividing the relative boundary mass by the square of the density. A boundary rectangle is completed and its absolute residual bounded by a sum of four nonnegative products. The principal terms retain both density factors by the full-content moments; the nonprincipal terms are covered by the same joint conductor estimate. Thus the small relative boundary weight can be used without the exponentially large inverse-density loss. The diagonal is bounded by the first moment and the proxy second moment. Thin pairs use the upper semismooth pair moment, not a pointwise independence assertion.

The written derivation is coherent at these vulnerable steps. We do not infer an independently certified asymptotic theorem from the finite checks.

## 3. Certificate audit and an economical replacement

Stark, *Some effective cases of the Brauer-Siegel theorem*, Invent. Math. 23 (1974), Lemma 8 p.145, gives the quadratic-subfield conclusion at gap \(1/(4a!\log|\Delta_L|)\) for arbitrary degree-a fields; the normal-field version has a different constant. Lagarias–Odlyzko (1977), Thm. 1.3, is for a normal extension **L/K**, with threshold \(x\ge\exp(10[L:\mathbb Q](\log|\Delta_L|)^2)\) and error with the possible exceptional zero plus \(x\exp(-c\sqrt{\log x/[L:\mathbb Q]})\). See the [accessible transcription](https://aareyanmanzoor.github.io/assets/articles/lagarias-odlyzko.pdf), pp.2,5. For odd-degree K and a nonrational square class, \(L=K(\sqrt\gamma)\) contains no rational quadratic subfield: such a subfield would generate L over K, making \([\gamma]\) rational. Thus the exceptional-zero gap is valid without assuming normality over Q or GRH. The choice \(S=(2d)!T^4,x=2^S\) gives \((1-\beta_0)\log x\gg T^3\) and satisfies the Chebotarev threshold. Degree-one ideals are sampled uniformly by the exact rational-prime/root procedure. Probe size is deliberately charged; it is not polynomial in n when d grows as a power of n.

The shared norm-parity and occurrence columns of E.6 are necessary: rational factors and the leading coefficient A must cancel under evaluation, and a field may have a bad prime that is good in another field. The derivative trace-dual formula places the integral root in the monogenic order, without computing its maximal order. The sealed CRT root distribution is invariant under the pre-verification public history. These arguments do not give a new workspace result.

For the next optimization use the unconditional source H.7 square-root argument rather than the very loose F.8 twelfth-power upper bound. We independently checked the cubic coefficient-height dependence of Lenstra–Lenstra–Lovász, *Factoring polynomials with rational coefficients*, Math. Ann. 261 (1982), Thm. (3.6), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01405454), [primary scan](https://www.math.ucdavis.edu/~deloera/MISC/LA-BIBLIO/trunk/Lovasz/LovaszLenstraLenstrafactor.pdf). A monic irreducible g of degree d and expanded nonzero \(\Gamma\in\mathbb Q[T]/(g)\) give
\[
 P_s(X)=\operatorname{Res}_T(g(T),(X-sT)^2-\Gamma(T)).
\]
Choose an integer \(0\le s\le2d(d-1)\) whose norm polynomial is squarefree; every pair of signed roots at distinct embeddings forbids at most one s. If \(\Gamma\) is not a square, the resulting element in the degree-2d field separates its 2d embeddings, so \(P_s\) is irreducible. If it is a square, there are two irreducible degree-d factors. The exact gcd of either factor with the defining quadratic over K gives a linear factor, because the two roots at the fixed embedding are distinct and in the two different rational factors. Subtract \(s\beta\) and verify the resulting square root. Resultants can be computed by integer interpolation at O(d) small integer X values and fraction-free determinants; inverses and degree-two gcd by polynomial-size linear systems. All intermediate coefficient heights are polynomial in d times the expanded input bit height. Classical arithmetic plus LLL Thm.3.6 therefore gives \(\operatorname{poly}(d)(1+h_g+h_\Gamma)^3\) bit cost. There is no need to factor auxiliary probe primes' p-1 or lift over enormous modulus for the square-root step. The argument works independently in every accepted field. It is unconditional even though H.7 is located inside the source's GRH appendix.

## 4. Search quotas: exact cost accounting

Another avoidable charge in U40's old ledger is assigning a recursive algebraic factorization to every raw proposal. At a good request the actual usable count is at least \(\tau/4\). If the one-point algebraic mean is at least \(p_A\) on every rationally eligible point, then \(\tau\ge p_A|R|\), and a uniform rational opportunity yields a complete descent or factor with probability at least \((7/8)(p_A/4)\). Here the \(7/8\) comes from the child-failure union bound; immediate factor exits can only help. At a covered top request use its pool counts and count each slot's rational points, including a failed slot as an unsuccessful proposal. The usable count is at least \(J\tau/2\), and the denominator of actual rational opportunities is at most \(J|R|\), so the same bound holds. Thus impose a separate local cap \(r_A=O(p_A^{-1}\log n)\) on algebraic factorization calls, while keeping the raw cap \(O(p_{\rm den}^{-1}\log n)\) and the verified-opportunity cap \(O(\log n)\). The proof is exactly the stopped-stream argument H.4; here **exact** recursive factoring replaces the source's RH-dependent recognizer. The cap counts unsuccessful algebraic calls and applies even to bad states. The failure probability is bounded by the corresponding geometric tail after virtual absorbing successes are inserted. No assertion about a conditional smoothness distribution after successful search is needed.

This separates the two leading call-count exponents, instead of charging the rational inverse density again for every expensive recursive call. All direct raw proposal work, rational divisions, field lookup and local frame copying still count. The new optimized ledger retains the old conservative copy charge \(P_{\rm raw}C^2\), so it does not rely on an unimplemented RAM access assumption.

## 5. Conclusion and scope

The audit did not discover a fatal mathematical defect in the inspected U40 proof chain. The main vulnerabilities (rare full contents, exceptional characters, small values in the large sieve, shared determinant, window boundary and certificate cost) have explicit checks above. The improved cubic root extraction and separate algebraic quota are reusable unconditional results; they remove major unnecessary cost terms. The next parameter schedule must independently check all range-dependent inequalities at its new degree, box and coefficient scales.

The existing covariance, U40 and U100 checks were rerun successfully in Python 3.13.5. The full repo/Lean/PDF build has not been rerun. The review does not certify external theorem proofs, all low-level arithmetic implementation details or the practical efficiency of the asymptotic algorithm. It does not show an algorithm below first L-parameter 1/3.
