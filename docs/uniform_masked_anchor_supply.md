# Uniform anchor generation by square masking and lattice coverage

27 November 2026. **Status:** proved distribution-free uniform anchor-supply theorem under a positive-mass low-rank representation hypothesis. It replaces the per-history missing-parity supply hypothesis in [joint ideal/image transport, §7](joint_ideal_image_transport.md#7-exact-binary-quotient-and-a-parity-progress-target) by a single sampling-law property. It does **not** establish that property at quarter scale for general factoring. The accompanying [square-resultant norm transport](square_resultant_norm_transport.md) gives a more economical representation interface in which no algebraic unit-log rank is charged for the bridged terms.

## 1. A legitimate enlargement of the anchor interface

Let \(N\) be odd, with primes and perfect powers handled as before. Let \(G\) be a public abelian multiplicative representation group with an evaluable homomorphism
\[
 \operatorname{ev}:G\longrightarrow(\mathbb Z/N)^\times.
\]
For example, G can be a product of the rational and localized odd-degree number-field multiplicative groups in the [exact unit certificate](exact_descent_unit_certificate.md), or the free abelian group on explicit public generators. Write its elements compactly. All evaluations, gcd guards and identities used below are verified. No private root is used by a representation algorithm.

A sealed row \(u_i\in G\) satisfies \(\operatorname{ev}(u_i)=s_i=h_i^2\). The independent uniform unit \(h_i\) is hidden; public computation can use \(s_i\), not the choice of its square root. In the previous transport theorem an unsealed anchor \(a_j\) had evaluation 1. Instead permit
\[
 \operatorname{ev}(a_j)=z_j^2,\qquad z_j\in(\mathbb Z/N)^\times
 \text{ explicitly known to the public computation}.
 \tag{1}
\]
The root \(z_j\) must be obtained without exposing any sealed root. It is not sufficient to know that the evaluation is a square, or to have a Jacobi-symbol test. The square roots \(z_j\) are stored as public correction data, not assigned new finite valuation directions. In particular one need not factor an integer representative of \(z_j\).

The extension of the earlier certificate is exact. If a public calculation finds integer vectors x,y with some \(x_i\) odd and
\[
 \operatorname{ev}\left(\prod_i u_i^{x_i}\prod_j a_j^{y_j}\right)=w^2
\]
for a verified known unit w, then
\[
 X=w^{-1}\prod_j z_j^{y_j}\prod_i h_i^{x_i}
\]
satisfies \(X^2=1\pmod N\). Conditional on the entire public transcript before private exposure, X is uniform on the unit square roots of 1, because at least one sealed exponent is odd. Thus a verified gcd split has probability \(1-2^{1-\omega(N)}\ge1/2\) for N with at least two distinct prime divisors. This also covers repeated prime powers. In the earlier exact terminal and log cancellation proof take w=1 after the combined sign equation. All those integer-kernel and Smith parity arguments continue to apply. This is a change in the *permitted witnesses*, not a claim that an anchor with nontrivial square image is literally an identity in the earlier representation group.

In particular let \(\nu:G\to P\) be the actual additive active incidence map to a free integer label group P, let \(V=(\nu(u_i))\), \(A_0=V\mathbb Z^k\), and let \(E\) be generated by the active vectors of anchors satisfying (1). The active parity criterion of the previous note remains
\[
 H\cap(A_0+E)\not\subseteq2A_0+E
 \tag{2}
\]
for an incoming batch after its integer new-label cancellation. The terminal and logarithmic rank budget must still be charged on the remaining exact kernel, unless a different verified square certificate removes it. Roots in (1) do not consume those ranks.

## 2. Distribution-free integer-lattice coverage

Here is the arithmetic-independent lemma that makes uniform masking useful.

**Lemma (novelty budget).** Let \(v_1,v_2,\ldots\) be any sequence in a fixed Euclidean space, possibly a finite-support coordinate space of initially unspecified dimension. Assume every vector has norm at most \(H\ge1\), that their real span has dimension at most R, and that every nonzero vector in the group they generate has norm at least \(0<\epsilon\le1\). Count a vector as novel if it is not in the **integer** span of its predecessors. The number of novel vectors, in every finite prefix, is at most
\[
 M=R+\left\lceil R\log_2(H\sqrt{\max(1,R)}/\epsilon)\right\rceil .
 \tag{3}
\]
For integer incidence vectors with the ordinary Euclidean norm the sharper bound \(R+\lceil R\log_2 H\rceil\) holds.

Proof. Each prefix group is discrete in its finite-dimensional span. Its covolume is the determinant in that span (the zero lattice has covolume 1). A new independent vector increases rank by one and multiplies the covolume by the length of its perpendicular projection, at most H. A new vector in the same real span enlarges the lattice by a finite index at least two, dividing its covolume by that index. There are at most R independent steps. By Minkowski's convex body theorem in rank r, the final covolume is at least \((\epsilon/\sqrt r)^r\), using the open cube of side \(2\epsilon/\sqrt r\) in orthonormal coordinates. This is at least \((\epsilon/\sqrt{\max(1,R)})^R\) for r≤R. Combining the upper bound from independent steps and the factors of two proves (3). For integer vectors the square of the covolume is a positive integer by the Gram determinant, so the lower bound is 1. The proof does not assume the span is saturated in a larger integer lattice, and counts index repairs at unchanged rational rank.

If a representation group has a finite torsion kernel of order at most \(2^\tau\) in its map to the Euclidean lattice, one may replace M by \(M+\tau\) for **group** membership novelty. Indeed at each novel step whose vector is already in the projected integer span, the new group supplies a nontrivial additional torsion coset, increasing the size of the subgroup of the torsion kernel by at least two. Such growth can occur at most \(\tau\) times. This proof also works after quotienting torsion by any fixed subgroup.

**Coverage theorem.** Let Y be an arbitrary random public representation with failure symbol \(\bot\). Let C be a set of possible successful outputs of probability mass \(\pi\ge p>0\). C is a proof device, not required to be recognizable. Assume its incidence vectors (or full representation-group elements) satisfy a novelty budget M as above. Outside C there is no rank or height restriction. Take m independent draws from the complete law Y, and let \(E_m\) be the integer span of all their successful vectors; failures add nothing. Then
\[
 \mathbb E\big[\Pr(Y\in C,\ \nu(Y)\notin E_m\mid E_m)\big]
       \le M/(m+1).
 \tag{4}
\]
In this expression Y is a fresh independent draw. With probability at least \(1-\delta\) over the sample bank,
\[
 \Pr(Y\ne\bot,\ \nu(Y)\in E_m\mid E_m)\ge p/2
 \quad\text{if}\quad m+1\ge 2M/(\delta p).
 \tag{5}
\]
The same statements apply to full group membership with the torsion correction in (3).

Proof. In a sequence of m+1 independent draws, a novel core output with respect to the full earlier span must also be novel with respect to just the earlier core outputs. Hence the number of these steps is at most M on every sequence, even if exceptional outputs introduce many completely unrelated directions. The missing-core-mass random variable is nonincreasing along a coupled growing bank, because its spans are nested. The expected number of novel core steps is the sum of the expected missing masses before each step. This proves (4). Markov's inequality bounds the probability that the missing mass exceeds p/2 by \(2M/((m+1)p)\), proving (5). This is an integer-lattice argument, not a coupon-collector estimate for a uniformly sampled finite label universe.

For binary incidence vectors the entire argument is simpler: take \(M=R\), because a novel vector increases dimension over \(\mathbb F_2\). The same coverage theorem holds with binary span. The [square-resultant transport](square_resultant_norm_transport.md) shows when such a binary presentation certifies a known square, rather than only an even ideal valuation.

### Implementing membership

For active integer vectors, exact Hermite normal form and integer linear solving give membership and a compact integer witness. For a finite product of the number-field groups in the earlier certificate, use the actual finite prime-ideal valuations and the full archimedean logarithms to define the additive coordinates. A nonzero valuation vector has norm at least 1. If all finite valuations vanish, the element is a tuple of units. The elementary separation proof in [the exact unit certificate, §4](exact_descent_unit_certificate.md) gives \(\epsilon=5^{-d_{\max}^2}/10\), with no asymptotic regulator hypothesis. The same separation holds for a direct product of fields; a nonzero coordinate is sufficient. Include the rational sign and all field signs in the torsion accounting, or quotient by torsion tuples whose combined evaluation is +1. In the latter case the torsion kernel has order at most two.

Full membership modulo that harmless sign subgroup can be computed without expanding compact powers. Apply the earlier Ge ideal-refinement and certified unit-log rational-kernel reconstruction to the bank elements and query together, then compute the saturated integer kernel. Impose the combined sign equation on that kernel over the integers modulo 2. Membership is equivalent to existence of a vector in the resulting lattice whose query coefficient is exactly 1 (or -1): the gcd of the last coordinates of a lattice basis must be one. Bezout gives the representation coefficients. This uses the previously stated polynomial-time arithmetic inputs, not an algorithm for finding a regulator or a maximal order. Bit costs are polynomial in the actual explicit row data and exponent lengths, not in the numerical sizes of compact exponents. For the binary generator presentation only finite binary linear algebra is needed.

## 3. Uniform supply for every missing target by a public square mask

Fix a public environment, including the permitted field presentations and a **capped** representation algorithm \(S(s;\xi)\). For a public unit square s and independent public coins \(\xi\), it returns a verified representation \(u\in G\) with evaluation s, a verified factor, or failure. S receives the square only. In particular its representation may not depend on the sampled root or the way the square was masked. This requirement is essential; using the trivial representation of a square as the square of its known sampling root would invalidate the argument.

Assume that for uniform unit-square input, the successful outputs contain a core of mass at least p and active novelty budget M, with respect to one common label universe. The terminal and log rank budget T needed in §1 must be valid after all retained anchor rows are included. The following construction is uniform in **every** old sealed square and its public history, subject only to that fixed sampling law and rank budget.

1. Prepare an unsealed bank of m independent uniform unit roots \(z_j\), pass just \(z_j^2\) to S with fresh independent coins, and store every accepted representation \(a_j\) together with its known root \(z_j\). The resulting anchors have (1). Treat verified factor exits as immediate success. The bank distribution in the no-exit analysis is understood by coupling to ideal uniform unit sampling and a sampler with a separate failure outcome.
2. Use \(m+1\ge2M/(\delta p)\). On an event of probability at least \(1-\delta\), (5) holds for the active span of this bank, with **no assumption about the old target incidence vectors**. This good-bank event depends on public data only.
3. Given any existing sealed row \(u_i\) whose active class is missing, i.e. \(\nu(u_i)\notin2A_0+E\), sample a fresh uniform public unit a and ask S to represent
\[
 s'=s_i a^2 .
\]
For every fixed \(s_i\) in the unit-square group, \(s'\) is uniform in that group. This holds conditionally on the entire previous public history, the bank, and any adaptive choice of the missing row i. The new sampler coins are independent. If the returned representation \(u'\) has active vector in the bank span, add the single anchor
\[
 b=u'u_i^{-1},\qquad \operatorname{ev}(b)=a^2.
 \tag{6}
\]
Its active vector is \(\nu(u')-\nu(u_i)\in A_0+E\), but is not in \(2A_0+E\). It has no new active coordinates outside the combined old and bank label group, since \(\nu(u')\in E_m\subseteq E\). Therefore this is a **strict** gain in the exact criterion (2), and makes the chosen missing sealed-coordinate parity available. The square correction a is known, without exposing \(h_i\). All newly created residual ideal terms are kept in the compact row expression; §2 of the joint transport theorem still couples their incidences to the same port exponents. Nothing asserts that a new residual prime ideal can be ignored simply because a modular mask is a square.

On a good bank each fresh attempt has conditional gain-or-factor probability at least p/2, uniformly over all reached histories with the fixed representation law. Growing E by adding more verified anchors cannot spoil that lower bound. If the active parity capacity is not already k, at least one standard basis vector of the sealed-coordinate binary space is missing. Therefore at most k successful masked gains are required. The exact integer membership and (2) tests are public. Once capacity exceeds T+1, the earlier terminal/log saturation and sign argument gives the split, now including all known square corrections. The good-bank event need not be decidable; use deterministic trial caps and reject a failed epoch.

**Uniform reusable mask bank.** One can even make the preceding statement simultaneous over every possible public square, not merely conditional on the next choice of i. On a good unsealed bank sample independent pairs (a,\(\xi\)) of uniform units and capped sampler coins. For each fixed s the failure probability in R' such pairs is at most \((1-p/2)^{R'}\). A union bound over at most N squares shows that
\[
 R'\ge (2/p)(\log N+\log(1/\eta))
\]
gives, with conditional probability at least \(1-\eta\), a mask/coin list in which every square has a covered representation. On this event the same list can be reused for all adaptively chosen targets, including repeated targets, because it works simultaneously. The program can test actual coverage without knowing that this event holds. Keep the environment and S fixed while reusing the list. This strengthens the uniformity conclusion without assuming that arbitrary deterministic small lists hit dense sets.

There is an alternative, stronger endpoint. If (5) holds for **full representation-group** membership modulo harmless torsion rather than just active incidence, then a single masked successful query is sufficient: its evaluation is a product of the bank squares with an explicitly known root, so division by a gives a verified square root of the original sealed square. No terminal/log rank constraint is needed in this version. All the previous known-root correction and membership checks apply. The private root was not used to compute that root, hence comparison by gcd has the same success probability.

## 4. Conditional quarter-scale complexity; averaging and exceptions

Put \(L=\log N\), \(\Lambda=L^{1/4}(\log L)^{3/4}\). Suppose, uniformly in N, the following bounds hold:

* A capped public representation sampler in a fixed usable environment has bit cost \(\exp(O(\Lambda))\), and its successful data have binary size at most \(\exp(O(\Lambda))\).
* A core of its raw uniform-square law has mass \(p\ge\exp(-O(\Lambda))\) and novelty budget \(M\le\exp(O(\Lambda))\). In the active version the common terminal/log rank bound T for all retained data is at most \(\exp(O(\Lambda))\), with the original sealed initialization of k>T+1 rows also available within this budget. In the full-coordinate version no separate initialization beyond sampling one independent sealed unit is required.
* All membership, verification and extraction costs are polynomial in the actual retained binary data (as for the explicit representation groups described above). Exceptionally large support/rank outside the core is allowed, but exceptional data size and per-trial work must still be capped.

Then preparation, coverage trials, and all exact algebra have total capped per-epoch cost \(\exp(O(\Lambda))\). For example take fixed \(\delta=\eta=1/16\), use the reusable mask list, and in the active version take k bounded as above. Product and polynomial factors of these scales stay at the same first L-parameter. Fresh epochs give constant success probability when the good environment is available, and strict smaller-input factor recursion or the verified outer factor tree retains the bound. A persistent trial-division backup gives termination on every tape.

The same reduction only needs an **averaged** environmental supply. Suppose each environment has the same cap and a core novelty bound M, with its core mass \(p_g\in[0,1]\), and \(\mathbb E_g p_g\ge p\) under a cheaply sampled public environment law. Then \(\Pr_g(p_g\ge p/2)\ge p/2\). Use the bank construction at lower threshold p/2 without identifying such environments. A fresh epoch has success probability at least a constant times p, provided the other stated initialization and rank guards hold in those environments. This changes only the leading constant in the quarter-scale bound. No selected-child conditional independence or lower bound at every rational prime is required. This is a different and weaker target than directly proving (2) for every adversarial history of a recursively selected request.

**Binary specialization.** For a public generator representation of a square, a vector of generator exponents modulo two is enough when there are explicit square-root correction data, not merely even valuations in an unknown ideal class. With a fixed binary generator universe, (4)–(6) apply with M equal to the core's binary dimension. New generators with even exponent sums can be canceled by their actual public values, with no integer-saturation defect. See the [square-resultant transport theorem](square_resultant_norm_transport.md) for a construction of such generators from ordinary polynomial norms and known modular factors.

**What is proved and what is missing.** The proof gives uniform generation of strict anchor gains from a single positive-mass low-rank core, including a reusable mask bank valid for all targets simultaneously. The input hypothesis is not itself a missing-parity probability statement, and no new anchor-specific equidistribution estimate is necessary. It is not established at \(L_N[1/4,O(1)]\) for arbitrary N. In particular small reduced ideal norms alone do not bound the full representation rank or supply a square-only sampler. Arbitrary norm-one lifts with a new independent field per sampled square can have rank equal to the number of samples. Nor can one substitute known-root-dependent representations for S. The existing U40/U14.1 arithmetic supply has a different scale and cannot simply be reinterpreted as a quarter-scale core. No new complete factoring bound is asserted.
