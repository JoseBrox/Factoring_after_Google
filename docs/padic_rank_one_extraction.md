# Quadratic rank-one extraction by exact 2-adic lifting

**Status (26 November 2026): proved exact algebraic extraction, new
necessary smoothness and short-relation restrictions, and checked
finite experiments. Uniform half-scale transverse-unit coverage is
still open.** The one-third program is deferred.

This continues [the one-field rank-one construction](one_field_rank_one_coverage.md).
We specialize to degree two to remove real-logarithm computation
from the extractor entirely. The existence of the requisite unit
relation remains a different problem from its exact detection.

## 1. Quadratic setup

For an odd composite N and an odd prime ell coprime to N, put
a=(ell-1)/2. Choose C>0 with
\[
 C\equiv-4\pmod N,\qquad C\equiv2\pmod4,\qquad \ell^2\mid C,
\]
large enough for the real-root guard in the earlier construction.
Let
\[
 f(X)=(X-2)^2+C(1+aX)=X^2+bX+c,\qquad
 b=Ca-4,\quad c=C+4.
\]
The field K=Q(theta) is real quadratic by the discriminant guard
and Eisenstein at 2. The power order A=Z[theta] has an evaluation
at theta=0 modulo N. The element
\[
 u=(1+a\theta)/\ell
\]
is an actual algebraic unit of norm 1 by the earlier integral
scaled-root proof. It belongs to A[1/ell] together with its
inverse, by the multiplication adjugate, and maps to ell^(-1)
modulo N. It is not a sign: otherwise theta would be rational.

The marks g_t=1+t theta have odd norm, since b,c are even,
so they are invertible in the 2-local power order. They all
evaluate to 1 modulo N. The same is true of the rational
marks (r+t theta)/r for odd r coprime to N, with r nonzero.
In general one can handle compact products of rational
power-basis elements whose rational denominators and
numerator norms are odd, using ordinary modular ring arithmetic.

## 2. Recover the exact unit coordinate without real logarithms

Let alpha be a compact product of the marks, with signed
integral exponents. Assume its ideal valuations are all zero;
this is checked exactly by the existing factor-refinement
matrix (or by actual prime-ideal valuations). Thus alpha is an
algebraic unit in the full integer ring of K. The product is
also represented in the localized power order and evaluates to
1 modulo N when the admitted marks do.

The unit-log lattice has rank one. Every nonzero vector has
length >1/3 by the norm-of-u^2-1 argument in the previous
notes. In particular, in either real embedding, the absolute
logarithm of a non-torsion unit is greater than
1/(3 sqrt(2))>1/5. Let R=2+max(|b|,|c|), a Cauchy bound for
both conjugates of theta. Set
\[
 J=6\,\operatorname{len}_2(1+aR),\qquad
 Q=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,J).
\]
Because u has norm 1, both its embedding absolute values
are at most 1+aR and at least (1+aR)^(-1). The unit-lattice
index of the subgroup generated by u (modulo signs) is
at most 5 log(1+aR)<J. Hence Q kills the quotient:
\[
 \alpha^Q=\pm u^k
\]
for a unique integer k. This is the same rational
coordinate k/Q recovered in the archimedean algorithm.
Importantly J is polynomial in the bit lengths of the
field data, and len_2(Q)<=sum_{j<=J}len_2(j)=O(J log(2J)).

For integral marks g, take a positive integer L bounding
log of every absolute conjugate of g. For example for
g=r+t theta, L=len_2(max(1,|r|+|t|R)) works for both upper
and lower bounds: the absolute norm is a positive integer
and the other conjugate is at most |r|+|t|R.
For a positive rational denominator s add len_2(s).
If S is the sum of the absolute compact exponents, then
\[
 |k|\le 5 Q L S.
 \tag{1}
\]
This uses log alpha only in the proof of the bound,
not in any computational step. All quantities on the
right can be obtained by exact integers; their *bit
lengths* are polynomial in the compact input.

### 2-adic order lemma in a finite free ring

Work in B=A tensor Z_2 with basis 1,theta. The reduction
of f modulo 2 is X^2. Since ell is odd, u reduces to
1+a theta there; therefore u^2=1 modulo 2 and u^4=1
modulo 4. Let v=u^4. Write
\[
 v-1=2^t w,\qquad t\ge2,\quad w\notin2B.
\]
The integer t is computable as the minimum coefficient
2-adic valuation of the numerator of
((1+a theta)^4-ell^4)/ell^4 in the power basis.
It is finite because u is not torsion. Its size is
polynomial in the bit length of the field data.

By binomial expansion, for all j>=0,
\[
 v^{2^j}=1+2^{t+j}w_j,\qquad w_j\equiv w\pmod{2B}.
 \tag{2}
\]
Indeed squaring gives w_(j+1)=w_j+2^(t+j-1)w_j^2.
If e is positive and odd, the leading coefficient of
(v^(2^j))^e-1 at precision 2^(t+j+1) is still w mod 2.
Consequently v has exact order 2^h modulo 2^(t+h)B
for every h>=1. This argument uses no field structure
of B/2B; freeness of B as a Z_2 module and the
nonzero vector w mod 2 suffice. It remains valid
for nonmaximal orders and ramification at 2.

Choose h with 2^(h-1)>5 Q L S, using bit lengths.
Compute y=alpha^(4Q) modulo 2^(t+h) by binary modular
powering of the compact factors, including inverses
for negative exponents. Then y=v^k.
Recover k mod 2^h as follows. Maintain a residual
y v^(-r), with r the bits recovered so far. At step j
the residual is 1 modulo 2^(t+j). Test whether it
is 1 modulo 2^(t+j+1); if not, add 2^j to r and
multiply by v^(-2^j). Equation (2) proves both
directions of the bit test and preserves the invariant.
After h steps the residue r is unique. The signed
representative in [-2^(h-1),2^(h-1)) equals k by (1).

**Theorem 1.** Given a compact actual unit in the
quadratic construction, with factors invertible at 2
as above, its exact rational coordinate in the unit
basis u is computable by deterministic integer and
modular ring operations in polynomial bit time in the
compact input size. No evaluation of real logarithms,
continued fractions, or numerical rank tests is needed.

For complexity, the modulus has t+h bits, polynomial
in the input, including the exponent bit lengths.
Each multiplication in the fixed-rank order has a
constant number of integer arithmetic operations with
polynomial-size operands. Inverting a numerator unit
uses the norm adjugate and the integer Euclidean
algorithm modulo the power of two. All powers use
binary repeated squaring, and there are h bit tests.
Q can be formed by successive integer lcm operations.
These are worst-case bit bounds.

If k=0, alpha is torsion. Otherwise reduce k/Q to
coprime numerator e and positive denominator q.
Equality of unit logarithms gives alpha^q=±u^e
(the reduction is justified by torsion-freeness of the
unit-log lattice). Since alpha evaluates to 1,
\[
 \ell^{2|e|}=1\pmod N.
\]
Verify it by modular powering. At a separating prime
the odd-part square chain gives a proper divisor.
Thus the old half-scale rank-one **decision and
extraction** cost is preserved, with no analytic
approximation in this quadratic branch. This does not
assert the existence of alpha.

## 3. First-jet sufficient condition on the valuation kernel

There is an even simpler integer-only *sufficient* test
for transversality. It applies in every degree with
monic irreducible f, c=f(0) nonzero and N|c.

Take distinct polynomial marks g_j with g_j(0)=1.
For an integral relation vector z put
\[
 M(z)=\sum_j z_j g'_j(0).
\]
Suppose the compact product is a sign. It evaluates
to 1 modulo odd N>=3, so the sign must be +1.
Clearing negative powers gives P(theta)=Q(theta),
with P,Q integral polynomial products of marks and
P(0)=Q(0)=1. Monic division gives P-Q=f h with h
integral. The constant terms imply h(0)=0, and
differentiating at zero yields
\[
 M(z)=P'(0)-Q'(0)=c h'(0).
 \tag{3}
\]
Thus the first-derivative residue mod |c| is a
well-defined homomorphism on the multiplicative
group generated by the marks. For normalized
rational marks with constant 1 and denominators
coprime to N, the same proof in Z localized away
from N gives a residue modulo N, with g'_j(0)
computed using the modular denominator inverse.

Let F be the exact valuation-refinement matrix.
Compute the saturated integral kernel of F by
integer normal form (a cleared rational kernel basis
may suffice for finding a positive certificate but
not for the exact modular-image assertion below).
For a kernel basis k_j, compute
\[
 D=\gcd(|c|,M(k_1),\ldots,M(k_r)).
\]
The image of the entire integral valuation kernel
under the derivative map is the subgroup D Z/|c|Z.
If D<|c|, some basis relation has nonzero first jet.
It is a genuine unit by the valuation equation
and is not a sign by (3). In degree two this is
a transverse relation, so Theorem 1 applies.
In higher degree a nonzero first jet need not be
transverse to the previously known congruence-unit
subspace; that implication is specific to rank one.

There is also a direct readout: if the gcd of any
computed moment with N (or the gcd of D with N)
is strictly between 1 and N, return it immediately.
If D=|c| the test is inconclusive: nontrivial units
can have zero first jet, e.g. after raising them to
the power |c|. Theorem 1 remains the complete
decision in degree two. Merely comparing ranks of F
and the augmented row modulo a prime is not a sound
substitute without checking saturation; an integer
kernel need not reduce onto the whole modular kernel.

This reformulates a sufficient existence theorem
entirely in terms of an integer matrix and a simple
mark row (t for g_t=1+tX). It eliminates any need to
prove a covariance estimate for an archimedean
logarithm row. We have **not** proved the required
nonzero image for every input.

## 4. Smoothness is unavoidable in the bounded quadratic linear family

One may hope that Ge's factor refinement, which avoids
factoring the individual norms, also eliminates the need
for a smoothness existence theorem. The following shows
why this is false for the fixed bounded linear family.

For the degree-two f above, the exact norm identity is
\[
 \operatorname{Norm}(r+t\theta)
   =r^2-brt+ct^2=(r+2t)^2+C t(t-ar).
 \tag{4}
\]
Let |r|,|t|<=H with log H=o(log N), and take primitive
pairs with r>0, so distinct pairs represent distinct
rational slopes. The two exceptional slopes t=0 and
t=ar give only rational scalars or multiples of the
known mark 1+a theta. For every other slope
t(t-ar) is a nonzero integer; hence
\[
 |\operatorname{Norm}(r+t\theta)|
       \ge C-9H^2\ge N/2
\]
eventually, since C+4 is a positive multiple of N.
The normalized mark (r+t theta)/r has absolute norm
at least N/(2H^2). This lower bound also holds for
larger valid choices of C. The exceptional normalized
marks are 1 and 1+a theta, whose latter norm is ell^2
and whose valuation vector is nonzero. They cannot by
themselves yield any nontrivial unit relation.

If a prime ideal above a rational prime p divides
both r+t theta and r'+t' theta, then p divides
r t'-r' t, a nonzero integer of absolute value at
most 2H^2. Thus a numerator norm with any prime
divisor p>2H^2 has an isolated prime-ideal row in
the whole mark family. Since denominators r<=H
are not divisible by such p, the row remains isolated
after normalization. Its coefficient is forced to
zero in every unit relation. For constant-coefficient
one marks with |t|<=H the sharper cutoff is 2H,
since the determinant is t'-t. No condition on
ramification or the index of the power order is
needed: reduction at the actual prime ideal gives
the determinant identity.

**Theorem 2.** In the bounded primitive linear-mark
family all marks usable in a nontrivial unit relation
must have numerator norms smooth at the corresponding
cutoff; at least one nonexceptional norm of absolute
size >=N/2 must occur. The constant-coefficient-one
version has cutoff 2H, not the general resultant
bound 2H^2.

Therefore the half-scale quadratic family cannot be
justified by a short-norm argument or by factor
refinement alone. Smoothness remains a mathematical
existence condition, even though computing the exact
relation space need not factor the norms. Adding
targeted marks outside the fixed height box could
bypass this restriction; the theorem does not
exclude such a descent.

## 5. A short-relation restriction

For distinct nonzero integer t_j of absolute value at
most H, let S=sum|z_j|>0. If the product of marks
1+t_j theta to powers z_j is torsion, the difference
P-Q from Section 3 is divisible by f. Every coefficient
of this difference at degree j has absolute value
at most 2(SH)^j. Suppose
\[
 2(SH)^k<|c|,\qquad 1\le k\le S.
\]
Induction on j shows all coefficients through degree k
are zero: the lower zero coefficients of P-Q force
the lower quotient coefficients to vanish because
c!=0, and the next coefficient is a multiple of c
of absolute value <|c|. In particular the first k
formal-log coefficients of P and Q agree, giving
the exact power-sum constraints
\[
 \sum_i z_i t_i^j=0\quad(1\le j\le k).
\]
This last identity is in rational formal power series
at zero, where log(1+tX) has its usual formal expansion.
If k is at least the number of distinct nonzero
t_i supporting z, the Vandermonde matrix for
powers 1 through that number is nonsingular (each
t_i is nonzero). It follows that z=0.

In particular if 2(SH)^S<|c|, no nonzero vector of
l1 length S can be a torsion relation. Combine this
with the density-one large-order lemma from
[the earlier obstruction](half_scale_unit_rank_obstruction.md).
For those balanced semiprimes and any polynomially
bounded target, an actual *nontrivial* congruence
unit has |e|>=ord_N(ell)/2 in the reduced coordinate
e/q. The index denominator is at most J=poly(n)
in the standard quadratic schedule (choose the
least permitted C above the earlier explicit root
bound, so log C=O(n+log ell)). Equation (1), or the
same estimate using q<=J, forces
\[
 S\ \ge\ \frac{\operatorname{ord}_N(\ell)}{10 J L}
          =\exp(\Omega(n))
\]
for polynomial-bit-height marks. This is a necessary
bound on the *values* of the compact exponents,
not their bit lengths, which may still be small.
Consequently the short torsion exclusion implies
that no nonzero valuation-kernel vector of l1 length
at most
\[
 \left\lfloor \frac{n}
    {8\log_2(2H(n+1))}\right\rfloor
\]
exists for sufficiently large such inputs, in
the bounded distinct nonzero linear family. Here
c>=N>=2^(n-1) and the displayed bound guarantees
2(SH)^S<|c|.

This explains why simple pairwise norm collisions,
short subset-product collisions, or abundance of
small kernel vectors cannot serve as the required
uniform argument. A successful compact relation
may need exponentially large exponent values,
even when each individual mark is small. None of
these restrictions rules out the full half-scale
search, whose integer elimination may produce such
exponents.

## 6. Finite exact verification

The [standard-library script](../verification/research/padic_certificate.py)
was run on the previous non-manufactured N=77 relation
search, with no use of its factorization to obtain the
marks. It verifies the ideal valuations at unramified
simple roots and the integer kernel equation, then
recovers the exact unit coordinate exclusively by
2-adic powering and bit tests. The earlier log-based
algorithm is not invoked.

    exact norm-gap checks: 40100

The 70 additional checks use known signed powers of the
explicit unit, including negative powers and exponents
of more than 300 bits, in five different quadratic
constructions. These test the lifting and signed
reconstruction, not relation existence. The 40,100
norm-gap checks verify (4) and its lower bound on
the nonexceptional pairs of a separate bounded box.
The first jet is nonzero in the actual extracted
N=77 relation, independently certifying that its
unit product is non-torsion.

## 7. What remains

The exact quadratic algorithm now needs only a
valuation-kernel product not equal to a sign.
One particularly concrete sufficient theorem would
show a nonzero first-jet image on the integer kernel
of a bounded smooth-norm family for some separating
target on every large N. A stronger complete route
could exploit targeted larger-height marks. Neither
has been proved here. The above restrictions show
why direct small-height or private-prime-free
arguments do not automatically give the existence
theorem.

**No unconditional general half-scale factoring
bound is claimed.** This is a new exact extractor
and necessary/sufficient structural analysis, not
a completed coverage proof or a new Lean milestone.
