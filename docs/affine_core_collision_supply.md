# Affine-core collision supply for correlated anchor batches

27 November 2026. **Status:** proved uniform reduction from *ordinary-unit*, correlated affine-core representations to strict sealed parity gains. The required collision mass and rank at quarter scale are not yet established for general inputs. The result is substantially weaker in its arithmetic hypotheses than a uniform anchor-generation assertion: the individual representations can have large and unrelated residual offsets, and need not represent squares. The companion [large-residual collision barrier](affine_tag_height_barrier.md) rules out a natural simple choice of tags.

This combines the [joint ideal/image transition](joint_ideal_image_transport.md), [known-square masked anchor supply](uniform_masked_anchor_supply.md), and [square-resultant norm transport](square_resultant_norm_transport.md). The principal objective is to exploit *matched correlations*, rather than demand individually smooth or low-rank terminal reductions.

## 1. Ordinary-unit representation interface

Fix N and a public environment, and write \(U=(\mathbb Z/N)^\times\), \(S=U^2\). The raw capped sampler \(A(u;\xi)\), for a public unit u and independent public coins \(\xi\), may return a verified representation of u in a common public multiplicative group G, a verified factor, or failure. It also supplies a public tag \(\tau\) when successful. The tags may be arbitrary discrete data, not necessarily prime labels. Representations may be signed compact field products, with verified modular evaluation. The binary actual-generator presentation of [square-resultant transport](square_resultant_norm_transport.md), §2, is also permitted: a representation of u specifies an explicit square correction and a binary vector of public unit generators, even if u itself is not a square.

As before all per-trial bit costs and output sizes are charged and capped. The sampler uses no hidden root or knowledge of the factorization of N. The full representation-group case requires the exact membership arithmetic stated in the earlier notes; the integer active-incidence case charges the terminal/log rank separately.

Let \({\cal C}\) be any proof-defined core of successful outputs. Let \(\nu\) denote their active or full representation coordinates. The key **affine** assumption is the following:

For every square coset \(D\in U/S\) and every tag \(\tau\), all differences of core vectors associated with inputs in D and tag \(\tau\) belong to a *common* subspace/lattice of rank at most R, with a common bound H on the norm of these differences and separation \(\epsilon>0\) for the lattice generated by them. Only the difference space must be common. The offsets for different \((D,\tau)\) may be completely unrelated and of arbitrarily high rank.

Equivalently within each such class the vectors lie in a translate of that common lattice, with bounded pairwise differences. In the actual-generator binary case require only a common difference subspace of dimension R; no metric assumption is needed. The core and the square cosets need not be recognizable computationally. The sampler uses only the public tag for equality testing.

For the transport of shared large ideal labels, the tag can record a common affine residual component of a block: two raw rows with the same tag then cancel that component on subtraction, while the actual residual ideals and modular images are coupled as in [joint transport](joint_ideal_image_transport.md), (5). The hypothesis is not that having a common ideal class or a common norm automatically ensures this cancellation. It must concern the actual incidence difference (or actual public generators in the binary presentation). In the full multiplicative-group case handle harmless torsion as in the mask-bank novelty lemma.

## 2. A ratio sampler gives the required square-only law

For a public square s, choose a fresh uniform public unit u and put \(v=us\). Run \(A(u;\xi_1)\) and \(A(v;\xi_2)\) with independent public coins in the *same* environment. If both succeed with equal tags, output their ratio
\[
 a=A(v;\xi_2)\,A(u;\xi_1)^{-1}.
 \tag{1}
\]
Its evaluation is s. In the binary presentation subtract the integer generator exponents and absorb the even parts into the explicit correction. Equality of tags is a public check. Factor exits are immediate successes; either other failure rejects this trial. This procedure is square-only: it does not use a root of s or its method of sampling.

Let \(g=[U:S]\). For each coset D and tag define
\[
 p_{D,\tau}=\Pr_{u\ {\rm uniform\ in}\ D,\ \xi}
       \big(A(u;\xi)\text{ is in }{\cal C},\ \operatorname{tag}=\tau\big).
\]
These are nonnegative subprobabilities. The total core mass of the ordinary-unit sampler is
\[
 p=\frac1g\sum_D\sum_\tau p_{D,\tau}.
\]

**Theorem (correlated collision law).** Under uniform s in S, the raw ratio sampler (retaining its pair data) has a core event of mass
\[
 \boxed{
 \Gamma=\frac1g\sum_D\sum_\tau p_{D,\tau}^{\,2}
 }\tag{2}
\]
whose vectors satisfy the common difference rank and height hypothesis of §1. In particular:
\[
 \boxed{
 \Gamma\ge p^2\sum_\tau \pi_\tau^2\ge p^2/T
 }\tag{3}
\]
if \(\pi_\tau=\Pr(\tau\mid{\cal C})\) and there are at most T tags in the core. The first lower bound uses the actual collision probability of the conditional tag law; it applies also to countably many tags and may be much stronger than the support bound. These formulas require neither uniform distribution within individual square cosets nor knowledge of those cosets.

Proof. Conditional on the coset D of uniform u, the pair \((u,us)\) is a pair of independent uniform elements of D, because s is independent and uniform in S. Conditional on those values the two sampler coin sequences are independent. Thus the probability that both outputs belong to the core and have the same particular tag is \(p_{D,\tau}^2\); averaging gives (2). Their difference is in the common difference lattice by the stated hypothesis, even if neither individual vector has small global rank. Jensen's inequality for the average over D gives
\[
 \Gamma\ge\sum_\tau\left(g^{-1}\sum_D p_{D,\tau}\right)^2
            =p^2\sum_\tau\pi_\tau^2.
\]
If T is finite, Cauchy–Schwarz gives the second inequality. End proof.

There is an immediate generalization: replace equality of tags by a public compatibility relation such that every allowed ordered pair of core tags in the same coset has its incidence difference in the common lattice. The exact mass then has the cross terms \(p_{D,\tau}p_{D,\sigma}\) over compatible ordered pairs. Equality is a sufficient and particularly simple version. It is not necessary to arrange a probability of success for every individual u or s. The bank and masking remove that requirement.

### Why the square coset conditioning matters

This construction is not the unjustified claim that independently sampled units from a dense set have a known square root of their ratio. The ratio's public square is chosen *first*. In a sealed trial sample h privately and use \(s=h^2\), then run (1) with public u; the pair \((u,us)\) and all acceptance decisions depend only on s and independent public data. Hence all h's in the unit square fibre retain their uniform conditional law. For an unsealed bank sample h publicly but pass only its square to (1). The bank representation has the known correction root h. The coset conditioning in (2) is a proof device, not a quadratic-character or Jacobi-symbol test used by the program.

A related pitfall is replacing (2) by \(p^2\) times a *uniform* probability of matching tags independent of the cosets. The two samples are correlated through their common square coset. The exact formula already accounts for this, and (3) follows by averaging rather than assuming independence before conditioning.

## 3. Uniform strict parity gain and quarter-scale consequence

Apply the mask-bank theorem to the ratio sampler (1). Its core novelty budget M is at most
\[
 R+\left\lceil R\log_2(H\sqrt{\max(1,R)}/\epsilon)\right\rceil
\]
plus the finite torsion allowance when applicable; for actual binary public generators take \(M=R\). Its raw core mass is at least \(\Gamma\). It may have successful outputs of high rank outside this core; those are not used in the novelty estimate. Preparation of \(O(M/(\delta\Gamma))\) unsealed ratio rows gives a bank covering raw mass at least \(\Gamma/2\) with probability at least \(1-\delta\).

For every old sealed square s_i, mask it by a fresh independent known square \(a^2\), run (1) on the masked square, and compare the resulting vector with the bank span. On coverage, subtract the old sealed row. The new anchor has known root a, and its actual active vector satisfies the exact integer progress criterion
\[
 H_o\cap(A_0+E)\not\subseteq2A_0+E
\]
whenever the chosen old parity was missing. Here \(H_o\) is the old-label contribution of the new anchor block as in the original criterion, not the height bound H. In the binary generator presentation use the corresponding criterion (5) of [square-resultant transport](square_resultant_norm_transport.md). The gain-or-factor probability is at least \(\Gamma/2\) on a good bank, uniformly over adaptively selected targets and histories. The simultaneous mask/coin-list version also applies. No additional per-target norm-smoothness, root-label coverage or quadratic-character estimate is required.

In the full-coordinate version a covered masked representation yields a known root directly, and no separate old sealed incidence matrix is needed. In the active version the earlier rank bound for all terminal/log directions must still be maintained; cancellation of the affine active offsets alone does not establish that bound.

Write \(L=\log N,\ \Lambda=L^{1/4}(\log L)^{3/4}\). Suppose the ordinary-unit sampler has cost and data size \(\exp(O(\Lambda))\), its core has total mass \(p\ge\exp(-O(\Lambda))\) and conditional tag collision probability at least \(\exp(-O(\Lambda))\), and its within-coset/tag difference novelty budget is at most \(\exp(O(\Lambda))\). Together with the preceding membership and rank guards, equations (2)–(3) and the uniform mask supply theorem give a conditional \(L_N[1/4,O(1)]\) factoring algorithm. Environmental averaging is allowed as in the previous mask note. It suffices to prove a positive-mass affine difference core on ordinary units; one need not prove a separate uniform square representation theorem or a uniform per-history anchor theorem. This is a **conditional reduction, not a proof of these arithmetic hypotheses**.

Compared with the preceding global low-rank-core condition, this is strictly more permissive as an abstract representation property: one may have many unrelated large affine offsets, no bounded absolute vector norm, or core support lying entirely in nonsquare cosets. Only the collision cost and the differences must be bounded. In an actual descent one can try to make the tag be a shared base norm packet, a coupled image/ideal offset, or a finer observed residual incidence class; the common difference lattice must then be verified and its rank actually bounded. This is the precise new way to exploit correlated requests instead of insisting that each local reduction has small rank.

## 4. A natural tag choice, and why it is insufficient asymptotically

For ordinary rational representations take, for example, the integer representative of u, factor it, and tag by its full outside-B prime part. Within a matched tag the difference of valuations is supported only on the small rational primes. This is a genuine simple affine-core sampler in finite experiments, and the difference rank is at most \(\pi(B)\). More generally use a primitive rational representation \(u=x/y\bmod N\) from a short determinant-scale lattice, and tag by its complete signed outside-B valuation vector. Matching tags cancels every such large rational residual even when neither representation is individually smooth.

The difficulty is the collision mass, not the validity of the cancellation. The [large-residual collision theorem](affine_tag_height_barrier.md) proves that for these rational tags with determinant-scale heights, the uniform same-square-coset collision mass is much smaller than the quarter-scale budget on balanced semiprimes. Its more general fixed-field version also excludes matching the entire outside-base ideal vector in the small-degree, bounded-log-height regime. Consequently the next viable use of (2) must exploit a *coarser* shared offset whose cancellation leaves a genuinely controlled residual **span**, or allow a materially different height/field-family budget. Counting all newly introduced primes separately is not necessary for the new rank hypothesis, but assuming their dependence without proof would be unsound.

The exact finite tests and their scope are recorded in [the verification guide](../verification/research/README.md). No uniform lower bound on \(\Gamma\) for the general number-field descent has been established.

## 5. Exact finite check

The [tested script and receipt](../verification/research/README.md#uniform-masked-anchors-and-affine-core-collision-supply) enumerate 986,984 same-coset pairs in eight small rational fixtures. All 23,188 matched tags give verified rational representations supported on the small base, and the same-coset collision formula agrees exactly with the enumeration. For example, at N=1189 and B=7 the ordinary representation vectors have rank 193, but the matched difference space has rank 4; its collision mass is 4819/156800. Unsealed sampled banks and exact integer membership give uniform masked-mass checks on all 986,984 mask/target pairs and 1,046 verified roots. The tests use deliberately tiny integer factorizations and exhaustive mask choices, not a practical or asymptotic core-generation algorithm. The barrier in the companion note shows why this literal tag method is not a quarter-scale supply theorem.


## 6. Subsequent concrete square-aware sampler

The [paired norm affine lift](paired_norm_affine_lift.md) supplies a uniform representation of arbitrary ordinary units by two integer norms and an explicitly known square correction. Its specialization makes one norm squareclass fixed across all targets and expresses the other as a norm in a fixed monic algebra. This gives a concrete algebraic affine offset cancellation, without supplied square compatibility. However the projected finite batches have essentially full remaining squareclass rank and no smooth squareclasses at the tested bases. The [norm parity collision obstruction](projected_norm_parity_barrier.md) also excludes determinant-scale fixed-field low-rank cores even after passing from exact ideal valuations to rational norm parity. A new arithmetic supply mechanism for the remaining variable norm is still needed.
