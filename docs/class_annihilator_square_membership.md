# Witnessed class square membership from a supplied annihilator

28 November 2026. **Status:** exact conditional extraction subroutine, polynomial in the actual generator data and binary annihilator length, with a factor exponential only in the 2-rank. It is not a class-number computation or an annihilator-supply theorem. This is a complementary endpoint to [the large class-coset rank obstruction](quadratic_coset_rank_obstruction.md). It uses elementary finite abelian 2-group elimination; no claim of novelty for the abstract finite-group algorithm itself is made.

The reason to consider it here is that mixing large squared subgroups produces nearly full-rank rational norm rows, even on repeated targets. On the other hand the abstract class group modulo squares has small rank. One needs an explicit **square preimage**, not just a genus-character or square-membership assertion, to get a known modular square correction from this observation.

## 1. Input and goal

Use the compact guarded quadratic states of [compact packet walks, §4](compact_quadratic_packet_walks.md#4-compact-class-preserving-composition), in one fixed order with \(D=kN\) and \(4k<3N\) so minimum norms have the unit-or-proper-gcd guard. We can also work in a direct product of such orders using canonical form tuples. Let G be the finite abelian group generated by a given list of proper ideal classes \(g_1,\ldots,g_s\). Supply compact guarded representative states for those generators. Some designated generators represent independently sealed squares \(s_i=h_i^2\), with public known corrections; the others can be unsealed public generator states.

Assume a positive integer M is supplied such that \(g_j^M=1\) for all j. This assumption is **verifiable** by compact powering and canonical class comparison, and then M annihilates G. It need not be the exact class number, need not be factored, and need not be polynomial as a numerical value. All costs use its binary bit length. If the check fails, reject that supplied annihilator; any proper gcd exit along the way is success.

Write \(r=\dim_{\mathbb F_2} G[2]\). In a single quadratic order the previous elementary bound gives \(2^r\le9\cdot2^{\omega(D)}\). We seek a nonzero binary vector on the sealed targets and a word \(w\) in the generators whose class satisfies
\[
 w^2=\prod_i g_i^{x_i},\qquad x\ne0.                       \tag{1}
\]
The word can have large binary exponents but must be explicit, not just an existence assertion of genus theory. We show that if the number of sealed targets exceeds r, the problem is solved in \(\operatorname{poly}(s,\log M,2^r)\) class operations and polynomial bit overhead in the actual class arithmetic inputs. It does not involve \(|G^2|\) or the rank of rational norm squareclasses.

## 2. Split off the odd part without factoring it

Write \(M=2^t m\), m odd. Use the CRT projection exponents \(e_2,e_o\) modulo M, with
\[
 e_2\equiv1\bmod2^t,\quad e_2\equiv0\bmod m,\qquad
 e_o=1-e_2\bmod M.
\]
For \(t=0\) put \(e_2=0\). These are computed by the extended Euclidean algorithm. Then \(g^{e_2}\) belongs to the subgroup \(G_2\) of elements of 2-power order, and \(g^{e_o}\) to the odd-order subgroup. Their product is g, they intersect trivially, and they generate G together. The odd part of any g has an explicit class square root \(g^{e_o(m+1)/2}\). The exponent is an integer because m is odd; its square is \(g^{e_o}\) since the odd part is killed by m.

Only the coordinates of the finite 2-group \(G_2\), killed by \(2^t\), need to be determined. The rank of its cyclic decomposition is r, regardless of the possibly enormous odd part of the class group or the exponents of its 2-primary factors.

## 3. Exact cyclic basis by order-lowering elimination

Start with the projected generators \(g_j^{e_2}\). Store each as a word vector in the original s generators, using exponents modulo M (this preserves class identities). Find the order \(2^a\) of each nonidentity projected element by repeated squaring, at most t squarings. Its **top element** is \(g^{2^{a-1}}\), a nontrivial element of \(G[2]\).

Maintain selected elements \(b_i\) of orders \(2^{a_i}\), with independent top elements. Maintain the table of all binary products of the selected top elements, labeled by their unique binary coefficient masks. Its size is at most \(2^r\). Repeatedly take an unprocessed element of largest actual order \(2^a\).

* If its top is not in the table, select it. Double the table by multiplication by the new top. The two halves are disjoint, so top independence is preserved.
* Otherwise use the known binary expression of its top in the selected tops. Their orders are at least \(2^a\) because of the order-descending selection. Replace the element g by
\[
 g\prod_i b_i^{-2^{a_i-a}c_i},
\]
where the \(c_i\in\{0,1\}\) are the table coefficients. Its \(2^{a-1}\)-th power is now one, so its order has strictly decreased. Replace its word by the corresponding explicit word and return the new element to the pending list. Identity elements can be discarded.

The replacement preserves the group generated by the selected and pending list, because it is multiplication of one generator by powers of other retained generators. Every repair lowers a pending order exponent, so at most \(st\) repairs occur. Top-table extensions occur at most r times. At termination the selected elements generate \(G_2\) and have independent tops. Their cyclic subgroups are in direct product: if a product of their powers is 1 with any nonidentity factor, take the largest order \(2^b\) among those individual powers. Raising the relation to \(2^{b-1}\) gives a nonempty binary dependency among the independent top elements, contradiction. Thus the selected cyclic factors form an actual independent basis, with exactly r factors and explicit words.

This is exact equality testing in the group. It does not call a discrete-log oracle or infer independence from a floating-point order estimate. All word coefficients may be reduced modulo M; group identities are still valid. Binary word lengths are polynomial in s and \(\log M\). In the final modular square-root computation we compose the actual explicit words, rather than assuming an arbitrary reduction of exponents leaves the modular correction unchanged.

## 4. Extract all 2-primary coordinates

For any supplied element y of \(G_2\), the cyclic basis and its top table give its unique coordinates \(n_i\bmod2^{a_i}\), using at most t levels.

At a level a, assume all remaining basis factors have order at most \(2^a\), and y is represented by their unknown remaining powers. Raise y to \(2^{a-1}\); only the factors of exact order \(2^a\) contribute their tops with parity equal to their current unknown exponents. The top table gives these parities exactly, with no contributions from the lower-order factors. For each of the maximal-order factors whose parity is one, divide y by the current generator and record that bit in its original coordinate. All current maximal-order factors then occur to even powers; replace those generators by their squares. This lowers the maximal remaining order to at most \(2^{a-1}\). Their nonidentity top elements are the original tops, so the same table can be reused. Iterate down to identity. Reconstruct the original element using the recovered coordinates as an independent equality check.

Now for each sealed target generator compute the coordinate vector of \(g_i^{e_2}\), and reduce its coordinates modulo two. The resulting vectors in \(\mathbb F_2^r\) give the exact map to \(G/G^2\). This follows because every element of the odd-order subgroup is a square, and in a direct cyclic 2-group factor an element is a square precisely when its exponent is even.

With more than r sealed targets, find any nonzero binary dependence by ordinary exact linear algebra. Let \(g=\prod_i g_i^{x_i}\). The coordinates \(n_j\) of \(g^{e_2}\) are all even. Consequently
\[
 w=g^{e_o(m+1)/2}\prod_j b_j^{n_j/2}
\]
is an explicit class square root of g. Verify (1) by actual class operations. If the number of sealed targets is smaller, this procedure still decides whether their parity vectors have a nontrivial dependency; it does not assert one without a kernel.

The total number of class operations and word arithmetic is bounded by a fixed polynomial in s, \(\log M\), and \(2^r\). The top table is the only exponential-in-r enumeration. In particular there is no enumeration of the whole 2-primary group of size \(2^{\sum a_i}\), or of the odd subgroup.

## 5. Turn the class word into a known modular root

Compose the original compact states with the found exponents to obtain a state for w, and square that state. Its public target value is now an explicitly known square, even though w's class may be complicated. Independently compose the selected sealed target states to obtain the state for g. They have the same canonical class key by (1), hence the same minimum norm. Write the state identities as
\[
 b\equiv u^2c_w^2\equiv \left(\prod_i s_i^{x_i}\right)c_g^2
       \pmod N
\]
where u is the public target value of the state for w before it is squared. Thus \(u c_w/c_g\) is a known unit square root of the sealed-square product. Verify it by modular powering, before exposing a private root. At least one x_i is one, so the signed private product divided by that known root is uniform on the unit square roots of 1 conditional on the public success transcript. Proper split probability is at least one half for odd non-prime-power input. A proper nonunit guard encountered earlier is already success.

This gives a different conditional quarter-scale endpoint, even for **large** squared subgroups: it suffices to supply the initial compact states and a verified annihilator with binary length and generation cost \(\exp(O(\Lambda))\), while \(r=O(\Lambda)\). For a balanced semiprime in a fixed twist with bounded \(\omega(k)\), r is bounded by an absolute constant. This does not show how to generate M or the initial packets at the required scale.

One explicit sufficient **extra hypothesis** is that the odd exponent of the generated subgroup is B-smooth, \(B=\exp(O(\Lambda))\). Take an elementary class-number upper bound \(H_{\max}>|C_D|\), e.g. \(8D+1\) by counting reduced form coefficient pairs as in the compact walk note. Choose \(2^t\ge H_{\max}\) and put
\[
 m_B=\prod_{\substack{p\le B\\p\ {\rm odd\ prime}}}
                          p^{\lfloor\log H_{\max}/\log p\rfloor},
 \qquad M=2^t m_B.
\]
Every prime-power divisor of a smooth odd group exponent is included; the 2-part is also killed. The powers in the product can be computed by exact integer comparisons, and the product's binary length and sieve cost are \(\exp(O(\Lambda))\). One can always **test** this proposed annihilator on the actual generators and reject if it fails. No smooth-order density or guarantee for a suitable twist is asserted. This is a version of the usual smooth group-order strategy, not an unconditional improvement from smoothness of the individual reduced norms.

## 6. Exact finite verification and limitation

Run [verify_class_annihilator_readout.py](../verification/research/verify_class_annihilator_readout.py), Python 3.13.5, SymPy 1.14.0, using the earlier two class arithmetic/check modules in the same directory. Tested and committed Git blob 502cd9dc8c146d2ce45c5e091501254f28a0f90e, SHA-256 6283d20325b7f5282da9c7f7ace1516fb7476196bcef5c54c0150fb6be6993f2. It completed 250 exact abstract finite abelian group certificates, 1,502 order-lowering repairs, and 4,753 independent coordinate checks across all elements of smaller generated 2-groups. In each abstract fixture the 2-subgroup was also enumerated independently to check the product of the basis orders and equality of the top table to the full 2-torsion set.

Three finite quadratic fixtures use supplied class numbers by exhaustive form enumeration (not a fast class-number procedure), and explicitly factored small public square representatives. For N=899,10403,1022117 the supplied class numbers are 42,90,928; the generated 2-ranks are 0,0,1 respectively. All three constructed verified public square roots before private comparison and returned factors 29,103,1013, with no public guard exit. These tests are small examples of the conditional readout, not evidence that the smooth-annihilator hypothesis holds at quarter scale.

The necessary future arithmetic advance is thus different from a full-coset norm-rank bound: obtain a sufficiently cheap **witnessed class square preimage**, or generate enough genuine odd class relations/annihilators in a shared group. The raw genus rank does not supply these witnesses. Neither a uniform such generation theorem nor a sub-one-third factoring bound is proved. No full repository, PDF or Lean build was performed.


## Subsequent polynomial readout and partial annihilator generation

The [unitary-divisor theorem](partial_class_annihilators.md) replaces the top-element table by exact gcd-refined binary divisor coordinates for every odd \(D>4\), including nonmaximal orders. The earlier exponential dependence on the 2-rank is unnecessary in this case. For \(D=kN\) with odd coprime k, a mixed ambiguous divisor is a proper factor; absent such an exit, the actual constructed subgroup has 2-rank at most \(\omega(k)+\mathbf1_{D\equiv1\bmod4}\).

The same note constructs verified annihilators from collisions in \(G^E\) with sample count \(O(s+\sqrt{s|G^E|})\), and useful partial annihilated words with only one or two independent sealed parities needed at k=1 after killing the 2-primary part. The input-sensitive cost need not involve the entire original odd group. No assumption of small residual group size is inferred from this construction, and initialization remains a separate open problem.
