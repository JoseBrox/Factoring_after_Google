## 3. Finite-probability and algebraic checks

The proof of Theorem 4.1 uses true conditional means before initialization. With a parent weighted by \(a_i/\tau_i\) and actual incidences \(X_{\ell q}\), the density of the child incidence law relative to its conditional-mean law really is \(H_q/a_{i+1}\) (equations (3.4), (3.5), (4.1)). The same random environment and field occur in both laws. Integrating the candidate-deletion inequality first, followed by Cauchy–Schwarz, proves the claimed recursion \(\delta_i\le\epsilon_i+4M_iK_i\sqrt{\delta_{i+1}}\). This does not assume independence of children.

The depth estimate is sound for the *printed* q-star: \(q_*=2^{32}\), \(j=\log D/(96\log 2)+O(1)\), and \(A=4^{j+10}=D^{1/48+o(1)}\). A fixed-power error would indeed be inadequate. The factor \(4T^2\) in (4.4) absorbs the square-root recurrence, and \(A/2^j\) dominates the polynomial loss. A linear truncation alternative is suggested by the source's own GRH Appendix G.6 and developed for the unconditional experiment in the continuation.

Section D.2 uses a conditional variance bound for the field pool and a separate loss expectation. This accounts for rejection even when rejection and supply are correlated. Section D.3 and H.4 distinguish raw proposals, verified opportunities and children; all failed calls count. Their cap proofs follow conditional geometric/Chernoff tails, not an independent-children assumption. The density of good shapes is a ratio-counting argument over square classes, not an equidistribution assertion for an arbitrary set of good primes. The coprime fraction condition uses the input's verified B-roughness to control \(2^{\omega(N)}\); the initial square fibre cancels the number of square classes in the lower bound. H.5's multi-band shape count avoids assuming smoothness for an adversarial prime subset and has the improved shape exponent \(9/20\).

The field certificate correctly avoids constructing a maximal order or factoring an entire discriminant. For a primitive irreducible degree-d polynomial f of leading coefficient A, its monic root coordinate is \(\beta=A\alpha\), with \(g(T)=A^{d-1}f(T/A)\). Equation (E.7) and the occurrence parity justify extracting the rational norm square class without factoring A. For odd d, the norm is the identity on rational square classes. The integral root construction multiplies by \(g'(\beta)\); the trace dual of the monogenic order is \(g'(\beta)^{-1}\mathbb Z[\beta]\), which contains the maximal order. Thus an integral root of the cleared product is in the computable order after derivative multiplication. The Vandermonde and height bounds in E.4 are sufficient for uniqueness of a centered lift, and all reconstructed identities are checked. Appendix E.6 accounts for the rational correction of *every* inserted internal atom and uses even good-large-ideal valuations to justify zero-extending later discovered norm columns.

For E.3, the odd-degree argument excludes a quadratic subfield of a nonrational quadratic extension of K: if F/Q were such a subfield, oddness gives K intersection F equal to Q, so L=KF and its square class is rational. The cited factorial-degree Stark alternative is important; one must not substitute a normal-field-only zero-free theorem. The explicit prime bit length \(S=(2d)!T^4\) leaves large gaps in both exceptional-zero suppression and the Chebotarev range. The full class-group bound and class separation are analytic/algebraic imports, not verified by finite tests. The probe cost is not polynomial in n: in U \(\log S=(2/3+o(1))\Lambda\), and in G it is \((1/2+o(1))\Lambda\). The manuscript charges it.

The sealed private roots in E.5 are conditionally uniform by an actual measure-preserving CRT group action on the original fixed-width fair-bit blocks. This avoids the common error of proving marginal uniformity but not transcript invariance. The nonempty selected set is determined before exposure. Even for prime powers, the local roots of unity are exactly plus or minus one. Exact square verification and the persistent trial-divisor backup suffice for correctness independently of the probabilistic analysis. Section 6's recursive expected-work argument charges fresh smaller-input calls conditional on the actual chosen input and does not multiply the auxiliary recursion tree twice.

## 4. Principal analytic claims checked against references

* **GRH smooth-character bound G.2.** Lagarias and Soundararajan, *Counting Smooth Solutions to the Equation A+B=C*, PLMS 104 (2012), 770–798, [arXiv:1102.4911v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1102.4911v2), Proposition 5.1 (preprint p.19, journal §5), supplies a truncated Euler-product bound uniform in y for primitive nonprincipal characters on \(\Re s\ge1/2+\eta\). The finitely many deleted Euler factors for an induced character cost \(O_\eta(k^\eta)\). Applying finite Perron at a half-integral endpoint and height \(x^2\) gives the stated \(O_\epsilon(x^{1/2+\epsilon}k^\epsilon)\) estimate. The principal-character term is explicitly retained after gcd division. This input is not a uniform prime progression theorem for the denominator.
* **Unconditional small conductors C.10.** S. Drappeau, *Sommes friables d'exponentielles et applications*, Canad. J. Math. 67 (2015), 597–638, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.4153/CJM-2014-036-5), Proposition 2.9, Proposition 2.13 and the exceptional term on p.619. The normal and exceptional cases must both be retained. At modulus equal to the exceptional primitive conductor, the Gauss prefactor in W cancels exactly, giving the smooth-character bound with the \(H(u)^{-\delta}\) factor. The source range \(Q\le y^{c/\log_3 x}\), \(y\le\exp(\log x/(\log_2 x)^4)\), and \(u\gg(\log_2 x)^5\) is compatible with the KeyBreak parameters. The fixed-exponent \(u^{3/4}\) estimate follows from the margins in C.10; I have not independently rederived Drappeau's contour lemmas.
* **Large conductors C.11.** A. J. Harper, *Minor arcs, mean values, and restriction theory for exponential sums over smooth numbers*, Compos. Math. 152 (2016), 1121–1158, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1112/S0010437X15007782), [accepted manuscript](https://wrap.warwick.ac.uk/id/eprint/107457/), Proposition 2, (4.1), p.23 of accepted manuscript. Its common bounded coefficient sequence and its explicit lower threshold \(x^{-1/1000}\) are real hypotheses. C.11 handles the moving upper endpoint by random signs on dyadic blocks; this is valid because each sign realization is one common bounded sequence. A stronger, independently proved small-value handling via the additive large sieve is in the continuation.
* **Smooth intervals and dilation.** Hildebrand–Tenenbaum, *Integers without large prime factors*, JTNB 5 (1993), 411–484, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.5802/jtnb.101), Theorems 1.1 and 5.1 and Corollary 2.3; their direct short-interval result, not a subtraction of two global asymptotics, is required. The paper also invokes the saddle ratio theorem of Hildebrand–Tenenbaum (1986) and Drappeau's global dilation inequality [25, Lemma 1(i)]. These two exact statements are kept as source analytic inputs to the extension; they have not been independently re-proved in this audit.
* **Genus two under RH.** H.3 uses the published order-fibre lower bound of Lenstra–Pila–Pomerance, *A hyperelliptic smoothness test II*, PLMS 84 (2002), 105–146, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1112/plms/84.1.105), Thm 1.1, and Younis, *Asymptotics for smooth numbers in short intervals*, [arXiv:2409.05761v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.05761v1), Thm 1.3. The accessible Younis abstract confirms the RH interval exponent range, but the full version was inaccessible on this pass. The source's use of the difference of the counts at orders divisible by two and by four gives the required order with exact two-adic valuation one. The CRT sampling of eight residues, odd scalar and gcd-tested Cantor branches give the separation mechanism. A correct bounded procedure under RH is a separate theorem; the paper does not need a hypothetical exact factoring oracle in G.

## 5. What has and has not been resolved

The finite and algebraic portions inspected above have no identified fatal logical defect. Several bounds are extremely conservative, especially the fixed conductor ceiling, exponential field pool, single-band root shape and repeated square-root loss in U. They are opportunities for improvement, not counterexamples to the paper's stated theorem.

The remaining independent referee burden is substantial: reconstruct all finite local pattern counts and their error normalization in C.4–C.9, the common-determinant summation (C.55)–(C.69) with each induced conductor/content case, and the G.7–G.13 separate-period endpoint and weighted-variance transfer. Check the precise Stark/Chebotarev and finite arithmetic theorem hypotheses and the exact Younis version as well. This note does not assert a proof of either headline factoring theorem independent of the source. It also does not invoke an unverified claim about polynomial-time integer factoring. The original source and the narrower extensions are clearly distinguished in the repository's status register.
