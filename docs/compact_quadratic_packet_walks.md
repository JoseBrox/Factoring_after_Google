# Definite quadratic packet reduction and compact square-ideal walks

28 November 2026. **Status:** unconditional improved reduction for supplied factored packets, compact ideal composition with explicit known-square corrections, and a uniform class-coset sampling theorem. Exact pooled tests now give useful sealed parity directions on larger fixtures than the earlier cubic and normalized norm pools. Neither the initial packet factorization nor quarter-scale rank/density supply has been proved. The reduction uses classical binary quadratic ideal arithmetic; no new general factoring exponent is asserted.

This continues [the fixed-field glued packet construction](fixed_field_glued_packet_reduction.md), [normalized norm lattices](normalized_norm_lattice_supply.md), and [the uniform anchor theorem](uniform_masked_anchor_supply.md). Instead of adding a congruence of index N to an odd-degree field, use a positive definite quadratic norm that is already an explicitly known square modulo N.

## 1. Direct packet transition

Write \(D=kN\) for a positive integer \(k\) and work in the explicit quadratic order \(A=\mathbb Z[\theta]\), \(\theta^2=-D\). It has discriminant \(-4D\), regardless of whether kN is squarefree; the order need not be maximal. Its norm form is
\[
 {\cal N}(x+y\theta)=x^2+Dy^2\equiv x^2\pmod N.
\]
Let \(\mathfrak a\) be any supplied integral invertible A-ideal of index \(Q\), with \((Q,N)=1\). Its coefficient lattice, under \((x,y)\mapsto(x,\sqrt D\,y)\), has determinant \(Q\sqrt D\). Perform exact two-dimensional Gauss reduction with Gram matrix \(\operatorname{diag}(1,D)\); no numerical square root is needed. If \(\alpha=x+y\theta\) is the shortest nonzero vector, the reduced basis has squared lengths \(A_1\le A_2\) and absolute dot product at most \(A_1/2\). Therefore
\[
 3A_1^2/4\le A_1A_2-\langle b_1,b_2\rangle^2
             =D Q^2.
\]
Since \((\alpha)\subset\mathfrak a\), its integer norm is divisible by Q. Put \(b={\cal N}(\alpha)/Q>0\). We obtain the exact inequalities and congruence
\[
 \boxed{3b^2\le4kN,\qquad
 b\equiv Q c^2\pmod N,\qquad c=xQ^{-1}\pmod N.} \tag{1}
\]
If \(4k<3N\) then \(b<N\). In this range the unit guard needs no extra short-vector search: if \((x,N)>1\), it cannot equal N because that would imply \(N\mid b\). It therefore gives a proper divisor. Otherwise c and b are units. In particular this removes the nonunit-shortest-vector issue that complicated the earlier high-dimensional norm envelopes.

The ideal
\[
 \boxed{\mathfrak b=(\bar\alpha)\mathfrak a/Q} \tag{2}
\]
is integral, invertible, has index b, and is in the **same** proper ideal class as \(\mathfrak a\). To check integrality, use \(\mathfrak a\bar{\mathfrak a}=Q A\) and \(\bar\alpha\in\bar{\mathfrak a}\). The product identity holds for any invertible ideal of a quadratic order: locally it is generated by a single element, and its product with its conjugate is generated by that element's rational norm. The valuations of this rational generator equal the valuations of the index, by the multiplication determinant. Multiplicativity of the index for invertible ideals follows from the same local argument. Thus (2) also holds at primes of the order conductor, provided the actual ideal is invertible. It is not asserted for arbitrary noninvertible ideals. Equation (2) can be verified using exact two-dimensional integer HNF and integer division of the coefficient vectors.

For any odd rational request prime \(p\nmid kN\) with \((-kN/p)=1\), choose a square root r of \(-kN\) modulo p. The ideal \((p,r+\theta)\) has index p and is invertible: the two local roots are simple, or directly multiply the ideal and its conjugate to get \(pA\) using \(2r\) a unit mod p. At p=2 use \(kN\equiv1\bmod4\); the index-two ideal \((2,1+\theta)\) has product with its conjugate equal to \(2A\) by \(1+kN\equiv2\bmod4\). Roots at the *supplied* primes are computable by finite-field arithmetic (e.g. Tonelli–Shanks with randomized nonresidue selection); no square root modulo N is requested. Products and arbitrary positive powers of these invertible ideals are permitted.

This is a norm transition for a **whole factored packet** of compatible primes. There is one residual b, not one b per prime. In particular the norm bound is independent of Q, even when Q is much larger than N. There is no field-specific unit-log rank or unmatched modular image label.

## 2. A shared twist bank of bounded height

Supply a finite set of t distinct rational request primes \(p\le P\le N\), all coprime to N. The following uses the published theorem of Banks, Garaev, Heath-Brown and Shparlinski, *Density of non-residues in Burgess-type intervals and applications*, Bull. Lond. Math. Soc. 40 (2008), 88–96, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1112/blms/bdm111), [arXiv:math/0607692](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0607692). Its abstract and stated density range were checked: for any fixed positive epsilon, the proportion of nonresidues in \([1,H]\) is bounded below by a positive constant for sufficiently large prime p and \(p^{1/(4\sqrt e)+\epsilon}\le H\le p\).

Fix \(4/25>1/(4\sqrt e)\) and choose \(K\ll P^{4/25}\), with a fixed sufficiently large absolute constant to handle the theorem's finite exceptional range. One may use \(K=\max(K_*,\lceil P^{4/25}\rceil,3)\), where the fixed constant \(K_*\) covers all smaller primes. For every odd p≤P needing a nonresidue, a uniform integer k in \([1,K]\) has Legendre symbol -1 with probability at least a fixed \(\delta>0\). If K<p this is the cited theorem (choose a fixed epsilon strictly below \(4/25-1/(4\sqrt e)\)); if K≥p, complete residue blocks alone give probability at least \((p-1)/(4p)\). The bounded smaller primes are covered by the fixed constant. This is a genuine positive-density theorem; mere existence of one small nonresidue would not justify polynomial-time randomized selection.

Use k=1 for primes already satisfying \((-N/p)=1\). Sample further independent k from \([1,K]\) until every other odd request prime is covered by some \((-kN/p)=1\). Check these conditions directly using the known primes. A gcd(k,N) that is nontrivial is a proper factor for sufficiently large N since K<N. If 2 is requested, include k=1 or 3 as needed to make kN≡1 mod4; a gcd exit is again allowed. By the union bound, after \(O(\log(2t/\eta))\) random twists the probability of any uncovered prime is at most \(\eta\). The expected number until complete coverage is \(O(\log(2t))\), since the tail is bounded by \(\min(1,t(1-\delta)^j)\). Constants depend only on the fixed density theorem, not N or the supplied prime set. This is a shared bank, not a prime-dependent field for every occurrence.

Assign each occurrence of a prime to any covering twist. For any supplied factored packet, multiply all its assigned ideals in each twist and use (1). Every individual residual satisfies
\[
 \boxed{b\ll N^{1/2}P^{2/25}.} \tag{3}
\]
At P≤N this is \(O(N^{0.58})\), strictly below N eventually, unlike the guaranteed bound in the fixed cubic glued reduction. The packet may split into several twist groups; its total rational representation is the **product** of their residuals, not an integer obeying (3). Charge each individual residual, its correction, and its occurrence count. The algorithm is polynomial in the actual supplied prime-list data, log N and the finite ideal operations below. The density theorem gives expected polynomial bit cost for the twist bank; deterministic caps followed by a fresh bank give arbitrarily small failure probabilities. For bounded input exceptions no asymptotic conclusion is drawn from a failed guard.

The bit-height improvement is a reduction subroutine, not a small factor base: if the largest original request primes are already far below \(N^{1/2}\), this estimate need not contract their size.

## 3. One twist for an entire sparse packet

There is a complementary uniform result that avoids splitting a packet across twists at the expense of a larger multiplier. Let q be odd squarefree, coprime to N, with t distinct prime factors supplied. For each fixed rational \(0<\epsilon<1/4\), put \(H=\lceil q^{1/4+\epsilon}\rceil\). For any prescribed Legendre signs \(\varepsilon_p=\pm1\), the number of k≤H coprime to q with \((k/p)=\varepsilon_p\) at all p|q is
\[
 (1+o(1))H\,2^{-t}\varphi(q)/q, \tag{4}
\]
uniformly as q tends to infinity.

Here are the range details. We use the classical Burgess bound for primitive characters of cube-free conductor d, in the form
\[
 |\sum_{n\le Y}\chi(n)|\ll_{r,\epsilon'}Y^{1-1/r}d^{(r+1)/(4r^2)+\epsilon'}.
\]
It holds for every fixed positive integer r; for Y>d one can remove complete periods and use monotonicity of the displayed bound. See Pierce and Xu, *Burgess bounds for short character sums evaluated at forms*, Algebra & Number Theory 14 (2020), 1911–1951, §1C1, p.1915, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.2140/ant.2020.14.1911), [accessible PDF](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10484233). The cube-free range and loss were checked in its extracted text; the page-image request failed with HTTP 502. We use the classical one-dimensional theorem reviewed there, not the paper's multidimensional theorem.

Expand the sign indicator by character orthogonality on the t coordinates. The principal contribution before multiplying by \(2^{-t}\) is \(H\varphi(q)/q+O(2^t)\). Every nontrivial product character is primitive of squarefree conductor d|q, with the remaining coprimality condition modulo q/d. Expand that condition by Mobius inversion:
\[
 \sum_{\substack{k\le H\\(k,q)=1}}\chi_d(k)
 =\sum_{a\mid q/d}\mu(a)\chi_d(a)
             \sum_{v\le H/a}\chi_d(v).
\]
For \(a>q^{\epsilon/2}\), bound the inner sum by \(H/a\le H q^{-\epsilon/2}\). For smaller a its length is at least a constant times \(q^{1/4+\epsilon/2}\), so using d≤q, choose fixed r sufficiently large and \(\epsilon'\) sufficiently small to get \(O((H/a)q^{-c_\epsilon})\) for a fixed \(c_\epsilon>0\). Indeed the exponent after division by the interval length is at most \(-\epsilon/(2r)+1/(4r^2)+\epsilon'\). Each nonprincipal contribution is therefore \(O(2^t H q^{-c'_\epsilon})\). Summing the \(2^t-1\) nontrivial products gives relative error \(O(4^t(t+1)q^{-c'_\epsilon})=o(1)\). We used \(\varphi(q)/q\ge1/(t+1)\) and \(t=o(\log q)\), the latter uniform for squarefree q by \(q\ge(t+1)!\) (bounded t causes no issue). This proves (4), including the smaller-conductor and coprimality terms. No unjustified simultaneous independence in a short interval is assumed.

Set \(\varepsilon_p=(-N/p)\), so (4) supplies one k making every p split. Sampling and testing has expected number of draws \(O(2^t(t+1))\), with fixed finite-q exceptions handled by a full residue block (absorbed in the fixed bound). If \(q\le N\), k<N eventually, and the same proper gcd guard applies. Now one reduction gives
\[
 \boxed{b\ll \sqrt N\,q^{1/8+\epsilon/2},\qquad b\equiv q c^2\bmod N.} \tag{5}
\]
Thus for t=O(\(\Lambda_{1/4}\)) and q≤N this single-packet transition costs at most \(\exp(O(\Lambda_{1/4}))\) plus the supplied-input work. This does not claim that arbitrary packets or the initial sealed squares have been factored at this cost. Repeating (5) after factoring the smaller residuals gives a log-size *envelope* recurrence with fixed point \(L/(2(1-1/8-\epsilon/2))\), \(L=\log N\), tending to \(4L/7\) as epsilon decreases to zero. This demonstrates both a real contraction from the initial N-scale bound and its inadequacy for a quarter-scale terminal factor base. It is not a recurrence proving that all residuals actually have the necessary factor counts.

## 4. Compact class-preserving composition

An explicit reduced state is a triple \((\mathfrak a,u,c)\), with \(\mathfrak a\) integral invertible, index \(b\le(2/\sqrt3)\sqrt{kN}\), and verified unit residues
\[
 b\equiv u c^2\bmod N.
\]
The two-dimensional ideal HNF basis has entries bounded by b, hence polynomial bit length in \(\log(kN)\). Given states for u and v, multiply their ideals by HNF, of index \(Q=b_1 b_2\), and apply (1)–(2). On success, the new state has target uv and correction \(c_1c_2 x/Q\pmod N\), the same uniform norm bound, and ideal class equal to the product of the previous classes. The square-root identity is checked exactly. The state need not store the expanded integer packet corresponding to u. Index \(Q\) and all intermediate integer coefficients have polynomial bit length in \(\log(kN)\). Ordinary Gauss reduction is polynomial bit time in these rank-two data. Thus binary powering of an ideal state with an exponent E costs polynomial time in \(\log(kN),\log(1+E)\). No intermediate norm factorization is necessary.

For an arbitrary supplied invertible ideal \(\mathfrak j\) of unit norm q, reduce and then square its state. This gives a known-square step with target 1 and class \([\mathfrak j]^2\): absorb the known scalar q into the correction. Multiplying any state by any nonnegative power of these steps does not change its target residue, and changes its ideal class by the corresponding square class. In particular this gives an actual source of repeated-target representations **without** changing the modular mask or the field. Nonprincipal step ideals can yield different reduced norms, unlike principal Hensel depth scaling. It does not assert that every step yields a new norm squareclass.

## 5. Uniform sampling in a squared class subgroup

Let G be the subgroup of the proper invertible ideal class group of A generated by explicitly supplied invertible ideals \(\mathfrak j_1,\ldots,\mathfrak j_s\) with unit norms. Its square subgroup is generated by their squared classes. Fix any target state of class a. Choose independent integers \(e_j\) uniformly from \([0,T)\), and compose the target with the square steps to those powers, reducing after each composition.

For every \(0<\eta<1\), one can take
\[
 T\ge s(8kN+1)/\eta.
\]
Then the abstract output class is within total variation \(\eta\) of uniform on \(aG^2\). The computation has polynomial bit cost in the explicit step data, s, \(\log(kN)\) and \(\log(1/\eta)\), although the exponents may be huge as numbers.

Proof. The classical correspondence between proper invertible ideal classes of an imaginary quadratic order and primitive positive definite reduced forms of its discriminant applies also to nonmaximal orders; see Cox, *Primes of the Form \(x^2+ny^2\)*, Theorem 7.7, [text](https://www.math.utoronto.ca/~ila/Cox-Primes_of_the_form_x2%2Bny2.pdf). Each class has a canonical reduced form \((a,b,c)\) with \(|b|\le a\le c\), \(a\le\sqrt{4kN/3}\). Count possible integer pairs (a,b), with c determined: there are fewer than \(8kN+1\) classes. The order of each squared generator is at most this bound. A uniform exponent in \([0,T)\) reduced modulo its order has total variation at most order/T from uniform. Couple all s exponents, at total cost at most \(\eta\). The product of independent uniforms on the generated cyclic subgroups of an abelian group is uniform on the subgroup they generate, by the constant-fibre property of the product homomorphism. Equation (2) preserves the abstract class throughout, and (1) gives a known-square correction. There is no need to know the class number, compute generator orders, use an expander theorem, or enumerate the class group.

The statement is about the abstract sampled class, or **factor-or-class-event** probabilities. If a nonunit guard returns a factor before completing the walk, that is success; conditioning on the absence of such a factor could change the class distribution. Use the hypothetical exponent-class product to prove the probability bound, not an assertion of uniformity after discarding all factor exits.

Gauss reduction gives the minimum norm of an integral ideal in the proper class. In fact the primitive norm form \( {\cal N}(x b_1+y b_2)/[\!A:\mathfrak a]\) has discriminant \(-4kN\); its minimum value is the leading coefficient of its reduced form. Thus the norm of the reduced state is determined by the abstract class, even in a boundary case with multiple shortest vectors. Different classes can still have the same minimum norm. Canonicalization, if needed, uses exact ordinary binary form reduction and sign conventions; no ideal-class group oracle is required.

This gives a concrete conditional supply statement. For a fixed collection of twists and fixed step subgroups \(G_j\), a target packet with assigned components has a product of cosets \(a_j G_j^2\). Uniform sampling of independent square-step exponents gives near-uniform distribution on that product, with all component norm corrections known. A low-rank core of reduced-norm tuples of relative mass p therefore has essentially the same p as a computable repeated-target sampler; choose total variation error less than p/4. If a prescribed product of target classes lies in the product of squared subgroups, random square steps land at the identity tuple with probability the reciprocal of the product of their subgroup sizes, up to the TV error. At the identity the minimum norm is one, giving a known root directly. In particular a small subgroup would give a useful mechanism, but its size is **not** known to be small, and deciding that the target belongs to it is not assumed free. Large class groups can have extremely small mass on a chosen small norm support. Uniform class sampling alone is not a quarter-scale rank theorem.

Every ideal multiplication, conjugate-product identity, integer quotient, determinant, reduced norm bound and modular correction was checked. In each twist use up to four known small split-prime ideals as square steps. For each target generate 48 square-walk trials with binary exponents of length \(2\operatorname{bitlength}N+8\). Form the product of residual norms across its twist groups, check all public corrections, and deduplicate by target and norm. Exact factorization of tiny residuals is used solely for the test matrix; the general parity certificate can instead use gcd-free squareclass refinement as before. **Next mathematical target.** The new compact class walk solves the repeated-target *generation* and uniformity part for a fixed squared subgroup, rather than relying on repeated short-vector searches in nearly equivalent lattices. It still leaves the distribution of projected residual norm squareclasses in those class cosets. A quarter-scale theorem needs a joint bound on useful parity rank or low-rank core mass at that scale, together with a charged initial packet recognition procedure. The classical discriminant-size envelope alone cannot reach this scale. The separate [linear norm-pairing budget theorem](linear_norm_pairing_budget.md) explains why simply replacing the definite quadratic order by a larger square-norm algebra does not solve the envelope problem.

## 7. Subsequent coset-rank obstruction and witnessed readout

The [class-coset norm-rank theorem](quadratic_coset_rank_obstruction.md) resolves the proposed unrestricted rank question negatively for large, independently mixed square subgroups. For minimum norms \(n(x)\) with \(X=\lfloor\sqrt{4kN/3}\rfloor\), every affine parity space of quarter-scale dimension has uniform coset mass at most
\[
 (X/|H|)\exp(-(1-o(1))U\log U),\qquad
 U\asymp(\log N)^{3/4}(\log\log N)^{-3/4}.
\]
The bound includes even exponents, arbitrary shared rational prime labels and adaptive offsets. Tatuzawa's effective class-size theorem and an elementary order-2 form count imply that for nonexceptional squarefree inputs with the stated small prime-count guard, full squared cosets and near-full squared subgroups have exponentially negligible mass in such spaces. It also excludes rank gains from a capped sequence of fresh uniform class draws even if the same sealed target is reused. Intermediate class guard factors, deliberately dependent class moves, and sufficiently small or high-index subgroups are not ruled out.

In finite checks the first four small split generators give the entire squared subgroup in nine discriminants. The new [small-subgroup dictionary](quadratic_coset_rank_obstruction.md#5-a-constructive-alternative-for-genuinely-small-squared-subgroups) supplies explicit square-correction witnesses when the generated squared subgroup times its 2-torsion budget is small enough. More generally the [supplied-annihilator theorem](class_annihilator_square_membership.md) computes witnessed class square preimages without enumerating the squared subgroup or factoring reduced norms. Both are conditional algorithms requiring their stated input supply; no general quarter-scale bound follows. The next viable class route is genuinely correlated generation or cheap witnessed class-square/annihilator supply, not unrestricted uniform mixing.
