## 2. Multi-dependency blindness (proved)

Let G be any finite abelian group, h:G->G a homomorphism, H=ker h, and z_1,...,z_m independent uniform elements of G. Reveal w_i=h(z_i) and any independent random tape. Condition on this transcript and a transcript-measurable integer exponent matrix E of size r by m. For each row j, suppose a verified Y_j satisfies h(Y_j)=product_i w_i^(E_ji). Then the vector of quotients
(product_i z_i^(E_ji) / Y_j)_(j=1,...,r)
is uniform on a coset of the image of E:H^m->H^r. Indeed each z_i is independent uniform on its H-coset, and any homomorphism on a finite group maps the uniform law to the uniform law on its image.

In the square case h(z)=z^2, H is elementary abelian 2-torsion. If E has full row rank over F_2, the map on H^m is surjective, and the quotients are independent uniform on H. For G=(Z/NZ)^* with odd N and k>=2 distinct prime factors, H has 2^k elements even if N is not squarefree. A gcd with the certified square root splits N unless the quotient is the global +1 or -1. Thus r independent verified binary dependencies give conditional failure probability (2^(1-k))^r, exactly. This strengthens Lemma 5.3 (r=1), requires no independence of the selected dependencies from the exposed squares, and permits arbitrary input-conditioned blind algorithms.

If the remaining square-class obstruction dimension is <=s (s=2 in the source), collect s+r extra relations beyond the row bound. Find s+r independent binary nullspace vectors and test their 2^(s+r)-1 combinations by the exact algebraic root test. The good combinations form a subspace of dimension >=r, so retain r independent good combinations. For fixed r, this costs only a constant factor; r=O(ln ln N) costs polynomial overhead. All unsealing must occur after the list is fixed; a failure may not adapt the supposedly blinded transcript using already exposed roots. This also applies to the existing random-self-reduction, collision, and local-discrepancy programme.

## 3. A sparse rectangular kernel algorithm (proved reduction)

Let A be a d by m matrix over F_2 with m>=d+3 and s_A nonzero entries. It suffices to return three independent binary null vectors. We prove a randomized, always-verifiable algorithm using O((d+m+s_A) polylog(d+m)) bits and O((d+m)(d+m+s_A) polylog(d+m)) bit operations per capped attempt on a multitape machine, with success probability bounded below by an absolute constant. The dense rank computation is unnecessary.

Work over F_Q, Q=2^h >=128(d+m), h=ceil(log_2(128(d+m))). A degree-h irreducible binary polynomial can be found by exhaustive enumeration in O(Q poly(h)) bit operations (irreducibility via gcds of x^(2^i)-x and divisibility of x^(2^h)-x), using polynomial(h) space. Let r=rank A. If A=0, return coordinate vectors. Otherwise choose independent random diagonal matrices D (m by m), E (d by d), with entries uniform in F_Q. Define the black-box d by d map B=A D A^T E.

The coefficient of z^r in det(I+zB) is the polynomial
sum_{|I|=|J|=r} det(A_{I,J})^2 product_{i in I} E_i product_{j in J} D_j.
This follows from principal minors and Cauchy–Binet. Some determinant is nonzero, and the monomials indexed by (I,J) are distinct, so the polynomial is nonzero even in characteristic 2. Its total degree is 2r. By Schwartz–Zippel, with probability >=1-2r/Q it does not vanish. On this event rank B=r, im B=im A, and the algebraic multiplicity of zero is d-r, equal to its geometric multiplicity. Thus F_Q^d=ker B direct_sum im B and B restricts to an automorphism of im B. The event need not be detected.

Choose a uniform v in F_Q^m and put b=A v. Solve Bx=b as follows. For b=0 put x=0. Otherwise choose a uniform row w and compute the sequence w^T B^i b, 0<=i<2d. Berlekamp–Massey gives its monic minimal recurrence polynomial f. If f(0)=0, reject. Else evaluate
x = -f(0)^(-1) sum_{i=1}^{deg f} f_i B^(i-1)b
and verify Bx=b. This is justified on the above good event: the true minimal polynomial of the Krylov vector b has degree <=d and nonzero constant term. If projection onto w loses a factor of that polynomial, then for at least one of its distinct irreducible factors g, w annihilates the nonzero vector (f_true/g)(B)b. The probability of any such loss is <=d/Q. A length-2d prefix suffices to recover the infinite minimal recurrence: both orders are <=d, and the difference sequence for a proposed shorter annihilator obeys the true recurrence, so d initial zero terms imply all later zeros. Berlekamp–Massey uses O(d^2) field operations and O(d) field elements of memory.

On successful verification put k=v-D A^T E x and verify A k=0. On the good event, with the true solution x in im B, this is a linear projection onto ker A, and is the identity there. Thus for uniform v it is uniform on ker A over F_Q. In a fixed F_2-basis of F_Q, its h component vectors are independent uniform elements of ker_{F_2} A, since A is binary. Their rank is at least 3 except with probability <=7*2^(-h): project to any three-dimensional quotient of the binary kernel and union-bound over its seven nonzero linear forms. Extract three independent components, verify A times each equals zero, and verify independence. Union-bound the bad preconditioner, projected recurrence loss and insufficient rank; success exceeds 3/4 for the chosen Q. This probability calculation compares the algorithm to the exact good-event projection without conditioning uniformity on successful verification. A false success is impossible; unsuccessful trials may be capped, restarting the outer factoring phase. Fixed finite loops and certificate verification suffice for a deterministic worst-case space bound.

Complexity: B uses one multiplication by each of A and A^T and two diagonal multiplications. On a multitape machine sparse matrix-vector products can be performed by sorting index/value records, merge-joining against the vector, and sorting/accumulating by output index. Each call costs O((d+m+s_A) polylog(d+m)) bits of work and memory; use standard sequential merge sort, not constant-cost random-access assumptions. The sequence and replay of the polynomial in B use O(d) calls. Field multiplication/division can use schoolbook polynomial arithmetic with poly(h) cost. Berlekamp–Massey's polynomial updates can be performed by sequential scans. Testing a fixed number of independent binary vectors costs linear work times h and verifying A products costs sparse work. The stated bounds follow. No full dense B is formed.

This is a self-contained specialization of black-box/Wiedemann linear algebra to the source's rectangular deficiency requirement; no novelty claim concerning the classical technique itself.

## 4. Apply the sparse algorithm to the relation matrix

The source uses Drow=3+pi(B)+|F_1(B)|+|F_2(B)|+2Lch and Rtot=Drow+3, with B=L[18], degree poly(ln N), and a poly(ln N)-sized character list (character-prime bit sizes at most L[0.6]). Thus dimensions are L[18+o(1)] in the sense exp((18+o(1)) Lambda). Each accepted relation is assembled from <=2r0 exp((ln ln N)^2) nodes, each storing factor lists of length poly(ln N). Only these occurrences, the three scalar rows, and the polylogarithmic character rows can yield nonzero parity entries. The number of nonzeros per column is exp(o(Lambda)), so s_A<=exp((18+o(1)) Lambda). Store prime/ideal labels and signed factor lists sparsely; sort labels to obtain matrix indices. Do not allocate the source's dense parity vectors. Each sparse kernel attempt costs L[36] time and L[18] space. A fixed number of attempts gives a fixed success probability (or a constant factor expected cost with an explicit cap). The exact final square-root verification from Lemma 5.2 is retained.

Keep only the sealed roots of accepted trials, and the current trial's root temporarily. On rejecting a trial erase that root. This is legal by the blind transcript theorem; accepted indices and the kernel algorithm are functions of w's and independent coins, not of z's. The root array shrinks from L[21.1] potential slots to L[18] accepted slots. The descent factor lists, sparse exponents, and parity matrix fit in L[18] bits; the factor-base storage is O(dB ln N); sampled character primes and their arithmetic fit into L[4] bits by the source. Lemma 5.2 gives workspace poly(d) Bbit with Bbit linear in the factor-list length times poly(ln N), hence L[18] for any fixed number of dependencies, even with its conservative L[19] bound. All other per-phase storage is L[18] or less, including the trial-division fallback's counters.

Recursive calls operate on integers at most sqrt(N)/2, and workspaces are reused sequentially. Their depth is O(log ln N); for every fixed c>18, summing the per-level bounds exp(c Lambda(N_i)) is at most exp((c+o(1)) Lambda(N)). This proves the advertised L[19] deterministic workspace bound, conditional on the source local-space claims. All random loops, including field draws and preconditioner retries, have deterministic size/time caps per attempt. In the event of repeated failures the original deterministic interleaved trial division still terminates. Additional constant success loss only affects expected phases by an absolute factor, not the leading L-constant.

## 5. Sharpened cap and recursive time induction

The source Proposition 2.3(2), applied on the good setup event of Lemma 4.6, and the proof on pp. 47–48 give the probability of a valid ratio as at least
(1/8)(4 r0 ln Q0)^(-2r0) - N^(-1/2+o(1)).
Here r0=(3/2+o(1)) U with U=(ln N/ln ln N)^(1/3), more precisely r0=ceil(3 Uhat/2), and Q0^r0 lies within a constant factor of sqrt(N). Hence 2r0=3U+O(1) and 4r0 ln Q0=2 ln N+O(1), giving a lower bound exp(-3 Lambda-O(U+ln ln N)) where Lambda=U ln ln N. The union bound for descent successes loses only a constant factor. Therefore the cap in Definition 6.1 may be replaced by
Mcap=ceil(8 Rtot exp((3+delta_N) Lambda_hat)),
delta_N=(ln ln N)^(-1/2),
using deterministic dyadic approximations with error o(delta_N) (or any fixed sufficiently small positive delta). Since delta_N Lambda dominates U+ln ln N, the proof of collecting Rtot successful relations with constant probability still works. Mcap=exp((21+o(1)) Lambda).

With an induction bound for expected full factoring of M of exp((C+o(1)) Lambda(M)), the source's at most 2Mcap recursive calls on arguments M<=sqrt(N)/2 cost exp((21+2^(-1/3)C+o(1)) Lambda(N)). Its descent bound, at most exp(o(Lambda)) nodes, Ttry<=L[2.05] trials per node and arguments M<=N^(1/10), gives exp((23.05+10^(-1/3) C+o(1)) Lambda(N)). Polynomial overhead and absolute expected-phase factors are absorbed. Setup costs L[37], exact root extraction L[38], sparse linear algebra L[36] (even the old L[55] is below the recursion threshold).

Thus for fixed C>max(38,21/(1-2^(-1/3)),23.05/(1-10^(-1/3))) there is strict slack in every induction term. As in the source, choose an effective sufficiently large threshold and a finite multiplicative constant for all smaller N using the deterministic fallback. One can use a monotone majorant F_C(N)=exp(C Lambda(N)) times a polylog factor, and prove the bound for full Factor by strong induction, absorbing worklist length and absolute phase factors using the strict slack. In particular C=102 is valid:
21+2^(-1/3)*102=101.957453650...<102.
The limiting admissible coefficient is 101.793764139..., so this improvement is modest in time but substantial in space.

## 6. Why the stronger ECM replacement is not yet established

The top recursive calls factor random ratios whose accepted numerator/denominator are products of r0 primes near Q0, with ln Q0~(1/3) Lambda_{2/3}(N). Replacing these calls by a capped recognizable-smooth factoring routine with cost L[c], c<~34, and high success on the products of a density-1 subset of the eligible primes would remove the 2^(-1/3)C term. Then even the original cubic elimination would permit C=56 if c<=33, and with sparse linear algebra the descent recursion threshold 43.01648... becomes the leading target if c is small enough.

But the needed L[1/3] smoothness test is not supplied by standard rigorous ECM results. Lenstra and Pomerance, *A rigorous time bound for factoring integers*, JAMS 5 (1992), 483–516, [primary PDF](https://math.dartmouth.edu/~carlp/PDF/paper85.pdf), Theorems 7.1, 7.3, 7.4: a dense set of recognizable primes is available, but the test takes exp(O((ln y)^(6/7))) for a prime cap y. At y=Q0 this is a log-N exponent 4/7, not 1/3. More recent short-interval smooth-number existence (Jain, [QJM 77 (2026), 397–422](https://doi.org/10.1093/qmath/haag010)) still starts at exp((ln Q0)^(2/3+epsilon)), and an almost-all-integers existence result alone does not ensure the required density among primes or sufficiently many smooth curve orders. The necessary statement is a uniform quantitative prime-exception and smooth-order-density bound (or a different recognizable-smooth routine); it cannot be assumed from the heuristic L_p[1/2] ECM complexity.

The source's own Lemma 3.5 handles intervals of *relative* width at least L_N[1/3,-1/5], not Hasse intervals of relative width Q0^(-1/2). It therefore cannot be inserted unchanged. This is an explicit open target for hybrid descent / elliptic-curve / recursive-cofactor families, not a completed speedup.
